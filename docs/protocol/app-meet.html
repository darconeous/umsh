UMSH Protocol Specification

Contact Exchange

![NOTE] This section is an early work in progress and this protocol may change significantly.

Meet payloads (payload type 10) let two people standing together exchange contacts without typing an address. Each device announces itself under a session key drawn for the purpose, showing only its model and a color derived from that key. One holder asks, the other agrees, and both screens show a six-digit code. Each holder compares the codes and presses Same or Different. A device sends its own long-term key and node identity only when its holder presses Same.

Nothing a device sends before its holder’s Same identifies it beyond its model. The person standing there is the identification, and the code confirms that the two devices talking are the two in their hands.

Session Keys

A session key is a fresh Ed25519 key pair, used as an ordinary node identity for the length of one session and never again. Every Meet frame is sent from the session key with the full source key in the header, and every unicast is addressed to the other device’s session key.

A device MUST draw a new session key, and new nonces, for every exchange attempt. That includes trying again after an attempt ends in Not now, Busy, Different, a timeout, or a failure. When an attempt ends for any reason, the session key’s private half is erased.

A session key answers for nothing but Meet. A device MUST NOT accept or answer any other payload addressed to a session key: no node identity, no MAC command, no text. Meet unicasts addressed to a long-term identity are dropped.

Payload Format

+------+---------+------+------+
| 0x0A | VERSION | KIND | BODY |
+------+---------+------+------+
  1 B     1 B      1 B    var

VERSION is 1. Any change to a body layout, or a new kind, takes a new version. Every kind except Identity has a body of exactly the length given below. Identity’s body runs to the end of the payload. No kind is followed by anything: bytes past the end of a body make the payload malformed.

KindNameCarriageBody
0HellobroadcastMODEL (2 B)
1RequestunicastCOMMITMENT (32 B)
2AcceptunicastNONCE (16 B)
3RevealunicastNONCE (16 B)
4IdentityunicastKEY (32 B), node identity payload
5EndunicastREASON (1 B)

A Hello is carried in a broadcast with no flood hops. Every other kind is carried in an encrypted unicast between the two session keys, with an acknowledgment requested and no flood hops. A kind on the wrong carriage is malformed.

Hello

MODEL is the sender’s hardware model, a model identifier (big-endian, 0 when unspecified). A Hello carries nothing else. It lists the sender on the receiver’s Nearby screen, keyed by its session key.

Request

Sent by the device whose holder asked (A) to the device asked (B):

COMMITMENT = SHA-256("UMSH meet commit" ‖ S_A ‖ S_B ‖ N_A)

S_A and S_B are the two session public keys, and N_A is 16 random bytes A keeps until Reveal.

Accept

Sent by B when its holder agrees. NONCE is N_B, 16 random bytes.

Reveal

Sent by A on receiving Accept. NONCE is N_A. B checks that the commitment it holds equals SHA-256("UMSH meet commit" ‖ S_A ‖ S_B ‖ N_A). On a mismatch B sends End with reason failed.

Identity

+-----+---------------------------------------------+
| KEY | ROLE | CAPS | OPTIONS | [0xFF | SIGNATURE] |
+-----+---------------------------------------------+
 32 B   node identity payload, to the end of the frame

KEY is the sender’s long-term public key. The rest of the body is its node identity payload, read as follows:

  • ROLE, CAPS, then options until the end of the frame or a 0xFF marker.
  • If the end of the frame is reached, the payload is unsigned.
  • If a 0xFF marker is found, exactly 64 bytes MUST follow it, and they are the signature. Any other count, including none, makes the Identity malformed.

The signature is the ordinary node identity signature: Ed25519 by KEY over ROLE through the 0xFF marker, as in a node URI bundle. It is optional. A signature that does not verify under KEY makes the Identity malformed.

An Identity carries no session binding. A sender that passed along someone else’s key and payload could neither read what is sent to that key nor send as it.

A device sends its Identity when its holder presses Same, and the Identity stands for that Same.

End

Ends the attempt for both devices.

ReasonMeaning
0Not now: the asked holder declined
1Busy: the receiver is in another exchange
2Different: a holder pressed Different
3Left: a holder backed out or closed Add peer
4Timed out
5Failed: a frame inside the exchange was malformed or did not check out

An unknown reason still ends the attempt.

Code and Color

Both devices show:

CODE = BE32(SHA-256("UMSH meet code" ‖ S_A ‖ S_B ‖ N_A ‖ N_B)[0..4]) mod 1000000

as six decimal digits, zero-padded.

Each session key has a color. Its hue step is:

HUE = SHA-256("UMSH meet color" ‖ S)[0]

The color is the OKLCH color with lightness 0.75, chroma 0.12, and hue angle HUE × 360° / 256, converted to 8-bit sRGB:

  1. a = 0.12·cos(h), b = 0.12·sin(h).
  2. l' = 0.75 + 0.3963377774·a + 0.2158037573·b m' = 0.75 − 0.1055613458·a − 0.0638541728·b s' = 0.75 − 0.0894841775·a − 1.2914855480·b
  3. l = l'³, m = m'³, s = s'³.
  4. Linear sRGB: R = 4.0767416621·l − 3.3077115913·m + 0.2309699292·s G = −1.2684380046·l + 2.6097574011·m − 0.3413193965·s B = −0.0041960863·l − 0.7034186147·m + 1.7076147010·s
  5. Each channel x, clamped to [0, 1], is encoded as 12.92·x when x ≤ 0.0031308 and 1.055·x^(1/2.4) − 0.055 otherwise, then scaled by 255 and rounded.

All 256 colors fall inside the sRGB gamut. Implementations may precompute them as a table.

Exchange

  1. Both devices broadcast Hellos and list each other.
  2. A’s holder picks B’s row. A sends Request.
  3. B’s holder is asked. Agreeing sends Accept. Declining sends End (not now).
  4. A sends Reveal, and shows the code.
  5. B checks the commitment and shows the code.
  6. Each holder compares. Same sends that device’s Identity. Different sends End (different).
  7. A device that has both sent and received an Identity has exchanged.

The commitment is sent before either nonce, and each nonce only after the other side is bound: B sends N_B only while holding C_A, and A reveals N_A only after receiving N_B.

What a device does with a received Identity is up to it, except that it MUST discard it if its own holder presses Different or leaves.

Rules

  • An Identity received before the device shows the code is dropped.
  • An Identity whose KEY is the receiver’s own long-term key is dropped.
  • When both devices send Request to each other, the device with the lower session key (compared as big-endian unsigned integers) ignores the incoming one. The other drops its own Request and sends Accept without asking its holder, who already asked.
  • A Request arriving while the receiver is in an exchange, or after its session’s attempt has ended, is answered with End (busy).
  • A device sends no Hellos while an attempt is in progress.

Malformed Frames

A frame from the session key a device is in an exchange with, by unicast, is inside the exchange. Inside the exchange, any malformed frame—an unknown version or kind, a wrong length, a wrong carriage, or an Identity that does not parse or verify—ends the attempt: the receiver sends End (failed) and keeps nothing.

Any other frame is outside. A malformed frame outside an exchange is dropped and never answered. A device speaking a newer version is simply not listed.

A well-formed frame that does not fit the current state is dropped.

Nearby

A device sends a Hello when it opens a session, one more between 0.5 and 2 seconds after it first hears a session key it has not listed, and then periodically. The periodic interval is at least 5 seconds, scaled to the airtime of the current channel settings, and each one is jittered by up to a quarter either way. A row ages out after three intervals without a Hello.

Two devices of the same model whose hue steps are within 24 of each other (about 34°, wrapping around) are hard to tell apart. While idle, the device with the lower session key of such a pair opens a new session, with a new key and a new color.

Transmit Power

A device SHOULD send every Meet frame, and every acknowledgment of one, at −9 dBm, or at the least power its radio can transmit if that is higher. The two holders stand together; nothing farther away needs to hear them.

Linking a Name to a Key

A Meet Identity travels between session keys, not from KEY, so nothing but its signature links its payload to KEY. An unsigned Identity’s name and other claims are the sender’s word only.
