This UEP defines the adaptive forwarder: a node that forwards where it hears
no other forwarder and is otherwise quiet. It defines the checks and the
delay an adaptive forwarder adds to the
[forwarding procedure](uep-0013.md#forwarding-procedure), which overheard
copies count against its pending forward, its engaged hold, its battery rule,
its states, and how it presents itself.

Beside repeaters an adaptive forwarder adds nothing to the channel. Where
there are none, adaptive forwarders are the mesh.

## Terminology {#terminology}

**Adaptive forwarder**
: A node that follows the [forwarding procedure](uep-0013.md#forwarding-procedure)
  as a repeater does, except as this UEP says. Whether a node is a repeater,
  an adaptive forwarder, or neither is a local setting.

**Heard from its originator**
: A packet that carries no `FHOPS` or a `FHOPS_ACC` of zero, and no
  [Source Route option](uep-0015.md#source-route), empty or not. No forwarder
  has carried it.

**Beside**
: A transmitter is beside the adaptive forwarder when a frame from it is
  received on the [measured](uep-0013.md#measured) class at or above the
  [beside threshold](#beside).

**Group**
: The adaptive forwarder together with the nodes beside it, as
  [Groups](#groups) counts them.

**Engaged**
: Within the hold period after the adaptive forwarder carried a flood heard
  from its originator ([Engaged](#engaged)).

## Parameters {#parameters}

An implementation SHOULD use these defaults unless a deployment sets others:

| Parameter | Default | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| Beside threshold | −65 dBm | [Beside](#beside), [Groups](#groups) |
| Group window | 10 minutes | [Groups](#groups) |
| Group share | 3 | [Groups](#groups) |
| Engaged hold | 5 minutes | [Engaged](#engaged) |
| Battery stop | 10% of capacity | [Battery](#battery) |
| Battery resume | 15% of capacity | [Battery](#battery) |

A deployment that sets its own values keeps the battery resume level above the
stop level, and a group share of at least 1. A node treats a group share of 0
as 1.

## Forwarding {#forwarding}

Nothing on the air distinguishes an adaptive forwarder's copy of a packet from
a repeater's. It edits the packet as a repeater does
([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#edits)).

### Routed Hops {#routed-hops}

A [routed hop](uep-0015.md#routed-hop) is forwarded as a repeater forwards it,
without delay. A route that names the forwarder was chosen by the packet's
sender. Only a [low battery](#battery) stops it. The rules this UEP adds to the flood-only
checks and to scheduling do not apply to it.

### Flood Hops {#flood-hops}

An adaptive forwarder takes a flood hop through these steps, in addition to
those of UEP-0013 and the UEPs it introduces:

1. **Low battery.** It forwards nothing ([Battery](#battery)).
2. **Beside it.** It does not forward a flood whose transmitter stands beside
   it ([Beside](#beside)).
3. **In a group.** With several nodes beside it, it forwards only if a draw of
   its own says so ([Groups](#groups)).
4. **Otherwise** it schedules the forward in a later window than a repeater's
   ([Contention Offset](#offset)), and gives it up when it hears another
   forwarder send the packet ([Giving Way](#give-way)).

The steps take these places in the procedure:

| Step | Stage | Outcome when it declines |
|---|---|---|
| Low battery | [1. Eligibility](uep-0013.md#eligibility) | Decline |
| Beside | [3. Flood-Only Checks](uep-0013.md#flood-checks) | [Decline and record](uep-0013.md#stage-outcomes) |
| Group | [3. Flood-Only Checks](uep-0013.md#flood-checks) | Decline and record |
| Contention offset | [5. Scheduling](uep-0013.md#scheduling) | — |

A flood left alone by the beside or group rule is recorded as a forwarded one
is. A copy that arrives later, from a forwarder further off, is the same flood
and is not considered again.

## Beside {#beside}

A transmitter heard very strongly stands beside the forwarder: it hears what
the forwarder hears and reaches what the forwarder reaches. Forwarding its
flood would cover the same ground.

An adaptive forwarder SHOULD NOT forward a flood whose copy it received on the
measured class at or above the beside threshold. Nodes in one room then
forward nothing among themselves, and nodes spread over a wide area, which
hear each other weakly, are unaffected.

The rule needs a measured signal strength, so it does not apply to a copy that
arrived on a [shared medium](uep-0013.md#shared-medium) or
[point-to-point](uep-0013.md#point-to-point).

## Groups {#groups}

A flood that reaches a group standing together from elsewhere is heard by all
of them at once, and any of them would carry it to the same ground. A few of
them forwarding it is as good as all.

An adaptive forwarder counts the distinct nodes beside it: the sources of the
frames it received on the measured class, within the group window, that were
heard from their originator and at or above the beside threshold. It tells
nodes apart by their source addresses, and MAY compare only a prefix of each
and bound how many it counts. Its own transmissions do not count. The group is
those nodes and the forwarder itself.

In a group larger than the group share, it SHOULD forward the flood with a
probability of the group share over the size of the group, drawn for itself,
so that about as many of the group forward as the share. In a group no larger
than the share, it forwards as it otherwise would. The draw MUST come from a
source the group's other members cannot predict, such as the node's
cryptographic random number generator.

The draw is each node's own because giving way to the first copy heard fails
in a crowd: forwarders that start within one
[channel sensing](uep-0012.md#channel-sensing) attempt of each other collide,
and none hears a copy to give way to.

The rule applies whatever the copy's arrival class. A flood carried over a
shared medium, or over links to several nodes that stand together, reaches
them at about the same moment, as one carried over the air does.

## Contention Offset {#offset}

An adaptive forwarder's flood forward contends in a window of its own, which
opens when every repeater's has closed. To the delay
[Scheduling](uep-0013.md#scheduling) computes for its arrival class, `D_ack`
included where the [hold-back](uep-0013.md#ack-hold-back) applies, it SHOULD
add:

```text
offset = W_max + 2 × W_jitter
```

The offset is the latest moment a repeater that heard the packet measured may
still be waiting ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#contention-formula)). Every repeater
in range transmits first, its record is the one a trace carries, and adaptive
forwarders are ranked among themselves as repeaters are.

The offset applies whatever the copy's arrival class. A repeater beside the
forwarder may have received the same copy over a link of its own, and is
scheduled without delay; the offset lets it go first.

### Confirmation Timeout {#confirm-timeout}

With the defaults of [UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#contention-parameters), an
adaptive forward begins no later than `D_ack + 1.4 × T_frame` after the
reception, which is `1.65 × T_frame` with the suggested `D_ack` of
`0.25 × T_frame` ([UEP-0012](uep-0012.md#ack-protection)). Its airtime is at
most `T_frame`, so it ends by `2.65 × T_frame`, within a sender's
[`confirm_timeout`](uep-0013.md#confirm-timeout) of `2.95 × T_frame`.

In general an adaptive forward ends `W_max + 2 × W_jitter` later than the
latest repeater's forward, and the guard margin left within `confirm_timeout`
is `T_frame − (W_max + 2 × W_jitter)`: `0.3 × T_frame` at the defaults. A
deployment that sets its own contention parameters keeps
`W_max + 2 × W_jitter` below `T_frame`, so that a guard margin remains.

## Giving Way {#give-way}

This section replaces, for an adaptive forwarder's unaired flood forwards,
which copies [give way](uep-0013.md#give-way) and on how many it abandons.

### Copies That Count {#counted-copies}

A copy counts against the pending forward when it has the same forwarding
identity, was received on the measured class, and is at least as far along as
the copy the forwarder received:

- it carries no source-route hints;
- its `FHOPS_ACC` is at least one;
- its `FHOPS_ACC` is no smaller than that of the copy the forwarder received.

That is another forwarder's copy of the same reception, or a carry from
further on. Either shows a forwarder in range serving the neighborhood. Among
repeaters a flood arrives from several of them and none carries it further,
each holding it already, so the second repeater's copy is the evidence that
the neighborhood is served.

A copy heard from its originator does not count. That is the originator
sending again because it heard nobody carry the packet.

### Abandoning {#abandon}

An adaptive forwarder SHOULD abandon its pending forward on the first copy
that counts.

While it is [engaged](#engaged), a forward of a flood that reached it already
forwarded is instead deferred on the first two copies that count and
abandoned on the third, as a repeater's is, with its delay recomputed as
[Giving Way](uep-0013.md#give-way) defines, offset included. A flood heard
from its originator still ends on the first copy: another forwarder's copy of
it is all that was wanted.

Whether the forwarder is engaged is decided when the copy is heard.

## Engaged {#engaged}

An adaptive forwarder is engaged for the engaged hold after it transmits a
forward of a flood heard from its originator.

That forward aired because nobody else carried the packet, so the originator
depends on this node. The traffic returning to the originator may reach this
node as several repeaters' copies, the first of which would end the forward.

Each transmitted forward of a flood heard from its originator renews the hold,
measured from that transmission. Forwarding any other flood does not, nor does
a routed hop: neither says anything about the neighbor.

## Battery {#battery}

An adaptive forwarder whose battery is running down SHOULD stop forwarding,
routed hops included, at or below the battery stop level, and resume at or
above the battery resume level. Its battery is its owner's first. A sender routed through it
hears no [confirmation](uep-0013.md#forwarding-confirmation) and finds another
way ([UEP-0016](uep-0016.md#route-recovery)).

A forwarder on external power, one charging, or one that cannot measure its
battery is not held back.

When it stops, it gives up the forwards it has queued. One that has not aired
[releases its identity](uep-0013.md#releasing-key), and one that has aired is
not retried.

The battery rule declines only the forward. A forwarder held back still
records its own radio's copy as [Eligibility](uep-0013.md#eligibility)
requires.

## States {#states}

An adaptive forwarder is in one of three states. They are local.

| State | When | Forwarding |
|---|---|---|
| Standby | Otherwise | Routed hops, and floods as [Flood Hops](#flood-hops) describes |
| Engaged | Within the engaged hold | As standby, tolerating copies of a flood that reached it already forwarded |
| Withheld | The battery is low | None |

Withheld takes precedence over engaged.

## Identity {#identity}

An adaptive forwarder does not set the `REP`
[capability](uep-0011.md#capabilities), does not claim the Repeater or Bridge
[role](uep-0011.md#roles), and does not list
[Supported Regions](uep-0014.md#supported-regions). Those name a repeater its
neighbors can plan on. Its neighbors record an adaptive forwarder only as they
record any forwarder, when they hear it forwarding
([UEP-0016](uep-0016.md#peer-list)). Whom it answers with its own Peer
Repeaters list is defined by [UEP-0016](uep-0016.md#peer-repeaters-responders).

## Registry Considerations {#registry-considerations}

This UEP allocates nothing.

## Security Considerations {#security-considerations}

**Inflated groups.** Nothing a frame counts toward a group is authenticated. A
transmitter received at or above the beside threshold that sends frames under
many source addresses makes the group look large, and the forwarder carries
few floods. It costs the attacker a transmitter strong enough at the
forwarder, nearby or powerful, and the airtime to keep the group window full.

**Loud relays.** A transmitter received at or above the beside threshold that
retransmits a flood unchanged, before the adaptive forwarder's own forward
airs, makes the forwarder leave the flood alone and record it. Later copies are
then duplicates for the [cache lifetime](uep-0013.md#cache-lifetime). It needs
no forgery and costs one transmission per flood. A repeater would forward such
a copy as any other.

**Forged copies.** A forged copy that appears further along makes a pending
forward give way, as for a repeater
([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#security-considerations)). An adaptive forwarder gives
way on the first such copy, so one forgery suffices.

**Engagement and battery.** Floods heard from their originator that no repeater
carries engage the forwarder, and one per engaged hold keeps it engaged, so it
tolerates more copies and spends more airtime. Floods that only it will carry
spend its battery at about the rate an attacker spends airtime, until it is
withheld.

**Visibility.** An adaptive forwarder forwards only what it hears nobody else
carry, so it is silent where repeaters serve its neighborhood. An observer
cannot tell it from an ordinary endpoint until it forwards, and a node learns a
route through it only from a packet it carried
([UEP-0016](uep-0016.md#route-learning)).

**Battery.** That a forwarder stopped forwarding is visible to a sender
routed through it. It may reveal that the forwarder's battery is low.
