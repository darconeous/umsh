This UEP defines contact exchange: how two people standing together exchange
contacts without typing an address, using **Meet** payloads. Each device
announces itself under a **session key** drawn for the purpose, showing only
its model and a color derived from that key. One holder asks, the other
agrees, and both screens show a six-digit code. Each holder compares the codes
and presses Same or Different. A device sends its long-term address and
[node identity](uep-0011.md#payload) only when its holder presses Same.

Nothing a device sends before its holder's Same identifies it beyond its
model. The person standing there is the identification, and the code confirms
that the two devices talking are the two in their hands.

## Session Keys {#session-keys}

A **session key** is a fresh Ed25519 keypair whose public key serves as an
ordinary node [address](uep-0004.md#addresses) for one exchange attempt and
never again. Its keys are converted and agreed exactly as a long-term
address's are ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#keys)). `S_A` and `S_B` below are the
public keys of the two devices' session keys.

A device MUST draw a new session key, and new nonces, for every attempt. That
includes trying again after an attempt ends for any reason: Not now, Busy,
Different, a timeout, or a failure. A device holds a session key's private key,
and the pairwise keys agreed under it, only in volatile memory, and MUST NOT
write them to durable storage, to logs, or to any other medium that outlives
the key's use. When an attempt under the key ends, for any reason, the device
MUST erase them once no packet it sent under the key still awaits
acknowledgment, and sends nothing under the key in the meantime but
retransmissions of those packets and MAC acks. It MUST also erase them when it
starts a new session key or stops listing nearby devices without having begun
an attempt under the old one.

A session key draws its frame counters from its own sequence
([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#frame-counters)), and a device SHOULD start that
sequence at a random value. A device does not persist a session key's counter
or its peer's baseline: a restart ends the attempt, and the key is never used
again.

A session key answers for nothing but Meet. A node discards any payload other
than Meet that is addressed to a session key, MAC commands included, and
sends nothing in answer to one but the MAC ack its packet requests. A node
discards a Meet payload that arrives by unicast addressed to its long-term
address.

## Payload {#payload}

A Meet payload is a payload of [payload type](uep-0005.md#payload) 10:

```text
+--------------+---------+------+------+
| PAYLOAD TYPE | VERSION | KIND | BODY |
+--------------+---------+------+------+
      1 B         1 B      1 B   variable
```

- `VERSION`: 1. A change to any body layout, or a new kind, takes a new
  version.
- `KIND`: the [message](#kinds) the payload carries.
- `BODY`: that message's body.

Every kind but Identity has a body of exactly the length its definition
gives. Identity's body runs to the end of the payload. A sender MUST NOT send
a body of any other length, or bytes after the body.

### Carriage {#carriage}

A Meet payload is carried by [broadcast](uep-0005.md#broadcast) or by
[unicast](uep-0005.md#unicast), and never by group unicast or multicast
([UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#compatibility)).

A sender MUST send every Meet packet from a session key, with the FCF's `S`
bit set so that `SRC` is the full
[source address](uep-0005.md#source-address), and without `FHOPS`. A
receiver drops, with accounting, a Meet payload whose packet carries a node
hint as its source. Such a payload, like one that arrives by group unicast or
multicast, is never [malformed](#malformed).

- A sender MUST send a Hello by broadcast.
- A sender MUST send every other kind by unicast to the other device's
  session key, encrypted and with an acknowledgment requested (`UNAR`). A
  Request is the unicast answer to a Hello, as
  [UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#response-carriage) allows a response to a
  broadcast.

### Kinds {#kinds}

| Kind | Name | Carriage | Body |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | [Hello](#hello) | Broadcast | `MODEL` (2 B) |
| 1 | [Request](#request) | Unicast | `COMMITMENT` (32 B) |
| 2 | [Accept](#accept) | Unicast | `NONCE` (16 B) |
| 3 | [Reveal](#reveal) | Unicast | `NONCE` (16 B) |
| 4 | [Identity](#identity) | Unicast | `KEY` (32 B), then a node identity payload |
| 5 | [End](#end) | Unicast | `REASON` (1 B) |

### Hello (0) {#hello}

`MODEL` is the sender's [model identifier](#model-identifiers), big-endian. A
Hello carries nothing else. It lists the sender on the receiver's
[Nearby](#nearby) list, keyed by its session key.

#### Model Identifiers {#model-identifiers}

A **model identifier** names a kind of hardware, so that a person can tell
which listed device is the one in front of them:

| Model identifier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `0x0000` | Unspecified |
| `0x0001`–`0xFDFF` | A board |
| `0xFE00`–`0xFEFF` | A host platform with no fixed radio hardware, such as a phone |
| `0xFF00`–`0xFFFF` | Private use: hardware with no assignment |

The project assigns boards and platforms their identifiers in the
[Model Identifiers](registries/model-identifiers.md) registry. A device with
no assigned model sends 0 or a private-use value. Apart from
comparing two identifiers for equality, as [Alike Colors](#alike-colors)
does, this UEP gives a model identifier no meaning: a receiver that does not
recognize one shows the device without a model.

### Request (1) {#request}

Sent by the device whose holder asked (A) to the device asked (B):

```text
COMMITMENT = SHA-256("UMSH meet commit" ‖ S_A ‖ S_B ‖ N_A)
```

`N_A` is 16 random bytes that A keeps until [Reveal](#reveal). Each quoted
label in this UEP, here and in [Code](#code) and [Session Color](#color), is
its ASCII bytes, with no length or terminator.

### Accept (2) {#accept}

Sent by B when its holder agrees. `NONCE` is `N_B`, 16 random bytes.

### Reveal (3) {#reveal}

Sent by A on receiving Accept. `NONCE` is `N_A`. B checks that the commitment
it holds equals `SHA-256("UMSH meet commit" ‖ S_A ‖ S_B ‖ N_A)`. On a
mismatch, B sends End with reason Failed.

### Identity (4) {#identity}

```text
+-----+------+------+---------+--------------------+
| KEY | ROLE | CAPS | OPTIONS | [0xFF | SIGNATURE] |
+-----+------+------+---------+--------------------+
 32 B  node identity payload, to the end of the payload
```

`KEY` is the sender's long-term address. The rest of the body is its
[identity payload](uep-0011.md#payload) without its payload type, as in a
[node URI's identity bundle](uep-0019.md#identity-bundle). The signature is
the identity's ordinary [embedded signature](uep-0011.md#signatures), by
`KEY`. A sender SHOULD sign it.

The Identity is malformed if:

- `KEY` is not a valid address ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#address-validation)),
- the identity payload is one UEP-0011 discards, or
- the identity payload carries a signature that does not verify under `KEY`,
  or
- an end marker is followed by other than exactly 64 bytes, including none.

The identity payload is unsigned when its options block runs to the end of
the payload, and signed when the options block ends in the end marker. A
sender MUST NOT follow the end marker with anything but its 64-byte
signature. A bare trailing end marker, which
[UEP-0003](uep-0003.md#end-marker) otherwise accepts, is not accepted here,
so that a signed and an unsigned Identity never share an encoding.

A device sends its Identity when its holder presses Same, and the Identity
stands for that Same.

### End (5) {#end}

Ends the attempt for both devices.

| Reason | Name | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | Not now | The asked holder declined |
| 1 | Busy | The receiver is in another attempt |
| 2 | Different | A holder pressed Different |
| 3 | Left | A holder backed out or closed the exchange |
| 4 | Timed out | A [limit](#timeouts) passed |
| 5 | Failed | A frame inside the attempt was malformed or did not check out |

An End with a reason the receiver does not recognize still ends the attempt.

## Code {#code}

Both devices show:

```text
CODE = BE32(SHA-256("UMSH meet code" ‖ S_A ‖ S_B ‖ N_A ‖ N_B)[0..4]) mod 1000000
```

`BE32` reads four bytes as a big-endian unsigned integer. A device shows
`CODE` as six decimal digits, zero-padded.

## Session Color {#color}

Each session key has a color. Its hue step is:

```text
HUE = SHA-256("UMSH meet color" ‖ S)[0]
```

where `S` is the session key's public key. The color is the
[OKLCH](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/) color with lightness 0.75,
chroma 0.12, and hue angle `h = HUE × 2π / 256`, converted to 8-bit sRGB:

1. `a = 0.12·cos(h)`, `b = 0.12·sin(h)`.
2. The cone responses:
   - `l' = 0.75 + 0.3963377774·a + 0.2158037573·b`
   - `m' = 0.75 − 0.1055613458·a − 0.0638541728·b`
   - `s' = 0.75 − 0.0894841775·a − 1.2914855480·b`
3. `l = l'³`, `m = m'³`, `s = s'³`.
4. Linear sRGB:
   - `R = 4.0767416621·l − 3.3077115913·m + 0.2309699292·s`
   - `G = −1.2684380046·l + 2.6097574011·m − 0.3413193965·s`
   - `B = −0.0041960863·l − 0.7034186147·m + 1.7076147010·s`
5. Each channel `x`, clamped to [0, 1], is encoded as `12.92·x` when
   `x ≤ 0.0031308` and as `1.055·x^(1/2.4) − 0.055` otherwise, then
   multiplied by 255 and rounded to the nearest integer.

All 256 colors fall inside the sRGB gamut. An implementation may use the
table in [Appendix B](#session-colors) instead of computing them.

## Exchange {#exchange}

1. Both devices broadcast Hellos and list each other.
2. A's holder picks B's row. A draws `N_A` and sends Request.
3. B's holder is asked. Agreeing draws `N_B` and sends Accept. Declining
   sends End (Not now).
4. A sends Reveal, and shows the code.
5. B checks the commitment and shows the code.
6. Each holder compares the codes. Same sends that device's Identity.
   Different sends End (Different).
7. A device that has both sent and received an Identity has exchanged, and
   the attempt is over.

The commitment is sent before either nonce, and each nonce only after the
other side is bound: B sends `N_B` only while holding `COMMITMENT`, and A
reveals `N_A` only after receiving `N_B`.

What a device does with a received Identity is up to it, except that it MUST
discard the Identity if its own holder presses Different or leaves, and that
a signed Identity is treated as [UEP-0011](uep-0011.md#signatures) treats an
identity heard outside any frame. An unsigned Identity is not believed: a
device SHOULD NOT let it replace details held about `KEY` without its holder's
confirmation.

A device sends Request only to a session key it has listed, and takes up a
Request only while it has not begun an attempt under its current session key,
or as [Crossed Requests](#crossed-requests) provides.
Its holder may back out at any point, which sends End (Left) to the other
device.

### Rules {#rules}

- An Identity received before the device shows the code, or after it has
  received one, is dropped.
- An Identity whose `KEY` is the receiver's own long-term address is dropped,
  and the attempt continues.
- A Request from any session key other than the receiver's peer, while the
  receiver is in an attempt, is answered with End (Busy). The receiver's own attempt is unaffected.
- A device sends no Hellos once an attempt under its session key has begun.
- A device that receives an End from its peer ends the attempt and sends
  nothing in reply.

### Crossed Requests {#crossed-requests}

When each device sends Request to the other, the device with the lower
session key, compared as a big-endian unsigned integer, ignores the incoming
Request. The other discards its own Request and `N_A`, and sends Accept
without asking its holder, who already asked. The device with the lower key
is then A.

### Timeouts {#timeouts}

| Waiting for | Limit |
|---|---|
| Accept, after sending Request | 60 s |
| The asked holder's answer | 60 s |
| Reveal, after sending Accept | 10 s |
| The holder's Same or Different, after the code is shown | 2 min |
| The peer's Identity, after sending one's own | 2 min |

Each limit runs from the event it names. A device whose limit passes sends
End (Timed out).

## Malformed Frames {#malformed}

A Meet payload is malformed if its version is not one the receiver speaks,
its kind is unknown, its body has the wrong length or is followed by bytes, it
is a Hello that arrives by unicast or another kind that arrives by broadcast,
or it is an Identity that is malformed as [Identity](#identity) defines.

A unicast whose source is the session key of the device's peer in the
current attempt is **inside** the attempt. A malformed payload inside the
attempt ends it: the receiver sends End (Failed) and keeps nothing it
received.

Any other Meet payload is **outside** the attempt. A receiver drops a
malformed payload outside the attempt, with accounting, and never answers it.
A device speaking a newer version is therefore not listed.

A well-formed payload that does not fit the current state is dropped.

## Nearby {#nearby}

A device lists each session key it hears a Hello from, with that Hello's model
and the key's color. A Hello from a listed key refreshes its row. A row ages
out after three Hello intervals without a Hello. Once an attempt with a
listed key ends, the key is removed, since it is never used again.

A device sends a Hello when it starts a session key. While the device has not
begun an attempt under that key, it sends one more Hello between 0.5 and 2
seconds after it first hears a session key it has not listed, unless a Hello
is already due sooner, and further Hellos periodically. The periodic interval
`I` is ten times [`T_frame`](uep-0012.md#t-frame), and never less than
5 seconds. Each Hello follows the one before it by `0.75·I` plus a random
duration of up to `0.5·I`, so the interval is jittered by up to a quarter
either way.

### Alike Colors {#alike-colors}

Two devices of the same model whose hue steps are 24 or fewer steps apart,
about 34° and counted around the circle, are hard to tell apart. While it has
not begun an attempt, the device with the lower session key of such a pair,
compared as a big-endian unsigned integer, starts a new session key, with a
new color.

## Transmit Power {#transmit-power}

A device SHOULD send every Meet frame, and every MAC ack of one, at −9 dBm,
or at the least power its radio can transmit if that is higher. The two
holders stand together, and nothing farther away needs to hear them.

## Security Considerations {#security-considerations}

### Code Strength {#code-strength}

A device in the middle of an attempt sends its commitment before it learns
the other side's nonce, and cannot change its nonce after it learns it. Each
attempt therefore gives an attacker between the two devices one guess at
making both holders see the same code: a one-in-a-million chance, paid for
with an attempt that both holders see fail. The code shows that the two
devices talking are the two in the holders' hands. It says nothing about the
identities those devices then send.

Low transmit power limits who hears an exchange, not who can take part in
one. An attacker with a sensitive receiver or a strong transmitter can still
reach both devices, and the code is what defends against it.

### Session Key Erasure {#session-key-erasure}

A session key's private key and its pairwise keys are erased when the key's
use ends, as an ephemeral address's are when its
[PFS session](uep-0009.md#forward-secrecy) ends, and
the same care applies: a key that reaches swap, a log, a crash dump, or
memory freed without being overwritten outlives its attempt. A new key is
drawn for every attempt, so the rows heard in one attempt cannot be linked by
key to the next.

### Correlation That Remains {#correlation}

A Hello names a model, and a rare model narrows who is nearby. A node's
long-term address keeps sending its ordinary traffic while a session key is
in use, so an observer can correlate the two by timing and location. A
session key's frame counter, if it continued the long-term address's, would
link the two directly; a session key's own randomly started sequence
([Session Keys](#session-keys)) does not.

### Linking a Name to a Key {#linking}

An Identity travels between session keys, not from `KEY`, so the unicast MIC
that carries it authenticates only the session key. Nothing but its embedded
signature links its contents to `KEY`, and a receiver believes it as `KEY`'s
statement only when that signature verifies, as for an identity heard outside
any frame ([UEP-0011](uep-0011.md#signatures)). An unsigned Identity's name
and other claims are the sender's word only. Even a signed Identity is only
`KEY`'s own statement about itself
([UEP-0011](uep-0011.md#claims)): the code shows that it came from the other
end of the exchange, not that its name is true.

A signed identity is heard in the clear, so a device can pass along another
node's. It cannot read what is sent to that address or send as it.

### Nearby Is Unauthenticated {#nearby-unauthenticated}

A Hello's model and a session key's color are unauthenticated aids to picking
a row: any device can claim a model and draw a key of a chosen color, and
only the code confirms which device is which. Anyone can add rows, and a
receiver bounds its Nearby list. A new session key draws a Hello from every
device that has not begun an attempt, which confirms that they are present
and reveals their model. A Request from any key asks the holder and occupies
the device, with other Requests answered Busy, until the holder answers or
backs out or its limit passes.
