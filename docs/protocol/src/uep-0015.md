This UEP defines source routing: the Source Route option, the routed hops it
names and the edit each one makes, the switch from a route to flooding, the
acknowledgment rule while a route is still pending, building a route, and
budgeting the flood beyond it. The forwarding procedure these rules fit into
is [UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#forwarding-procedure).

## Source Route Option (3) {#source-route}

The Source Route option lists the repeaters a packet is to cross, in order.
It is forwarding-critical and dynamic
([UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#option-attributes)).

```text
+----------+----------+----------+-----+
|  RH[0]   |  RH[1]   |  RH[2]   | ... |
+----------+----------+----------+-----+
    2 B        2 B        2 B
```

Each `RH[i]` is a [repeater hint](uep-0004.md#repeater-hint), and the hints
appear in the order the packet crosses the repeaters they name. `RH[0]` names
the next repeater to forward the packet. A route names repeaters only.

- **Length.** A value whose length is odd cannot be interpreted. A packet
  carrying one is not forwarded, and its local delivery is unaffected
  ([UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#criticality)).
- **Singleton.** A sender MUST NOT include more than one Source Route option.
  A packet carrying more than one is handled by a repeater as carrying a
  forwarding-critical option it cannot interpret. A destination reads the
  first occurrence.
- **Empty value.** For choosing the [hop kind](uep-0013.md#hop-kind), an
  empty Source Route behaves like an absent one: there is no next hop to
  name. It still records that the packet crossed an explicit path, which
  [UEP-0018](uep-0018.md) reads.
- **Sender limits.** A sender MUST NOT originate an empty Source Route, and
  MUST NOT include more than 15 hints. An empty route would claim a path the
  packet never took.
- **Forwarders** accept a route of any even length that fits the frame.

### Matching the Route {#route-matching}

A repeater forwards under one address of its own. Its repeater hint, the one
it matches routes against, is that address's hint. A node that advertises
the `REP` [capability](uep-0011.md#capabilities) does so under the same
address.

Repeater hints collide ([UEP-0004](uep-0004.md#collisions)). Every repeater
that shares the named hint matches it and forwards as a routed hop. The copies
they send share a [forwarding identity](uep-0013.md#forwarding-identity), so
duplicate suppression downstream absorbs them.

## Routed Hops {#routed-hop}

A repeater whose repeater hint matches `RH[0]` forwards the packet as a
[routed hop](uep-0013.md#hop-kind). This includes the case where `RH[0]` is
the last hint. A repeater whose hint does not match `RH[0]` MUST NOT forward
the packet.

A routed hop skips the [flood-only rules](uep-0013.md#hop-kinds), leaves
`FHOPS` untouched ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#fhops-accounting)), and inserts no
region code ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#region-insertion)). It makes the
[routed-hop edit](#routed-hop-edit) and the other edits that apply to every
hop, such as Trace's ([UEP-0016](uep-0016.md)), and is scheduled under normal
channel access ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#scheduling)).

Which copy confirms a routed hop, and whether the hop that removes the last
hint listens at all, is defined in
[UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#confirmation-scope).

### The Routed-Hop Edit {#routed-hop-edit}

The repeater removes `RH[0]`, its own hint, from the Source Route. This is
step 2 of the [edits to the packet](uep-0013.md#edits).

If no hints remain, the repeater MUST keep the option with an empty value.
The forwarded packet then still shows that it arrived by an explicit route.

The edit never lengthens the packet.

## The Switch to Flooding {#route-end}

The repeater that removes the last hint was named, so its hop is routed: it
leaves `FHOPS` and region codes as they are.

The next repeater receives an empty route, has no hint to match, and so
forwards a flood hop. It is the first to flood:

- it pays the first flood hop ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#fhops-accounting));
- it applies region policy ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#region-policy));
- it is the first that may insert a region code
  ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#region-insertion)).

From there the packet floods within its remaining `FHOPS`. A packet that
cannot be flood-forwarded ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#fhops-accounting)), such as
one without `FHOPS`, goes no further than the last named repeater.

## Acknowledgment While a Route Is Pending {#pending-route-ack}

A destination processes a packet addressed to it whether or not hints remain
in its Source Route ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#receive-procedure)), so a node
that hears a copy early may accept its payload.

A destination MUST NOT acknowledge a packet whose Source Route option is not
empty. It acknowledges a copy that arrives with the option empty or absent.
The next named repeater forwards without holding back, so an immediate
acknowledgment would contend with it for the channel. The same rule applies to
[duplicate acknowledgments](uep-0007.md#duplicate-acknowledgments).

The first copy that arrives with the route consumed is the first eligible
for an acknowledgment, and the destination MAY acknowledge it at once:
accepting the payload from an earlier copy does not start the
[pacing window](uep-0013.md#ack-pacing).

So a packet received with hints pending draws no
[immediate acknowledgment](uep-0012.md#immediate-ack), and the
[acknowledgment protection interval](uep-0012.md#ack-protection) never applies
to forwarding it ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#ack-hold-back)).

## Building a Source Route {#building-routes}

A sender lists the hints first hop first, repeaters only, within the
[sender limits](#source-route). A sender MUST NOT name the destination in a
Source Route: the destination neither forwards the packet nor acknowledges it
while hints remain. Where routes come from, and when a node uses one, is
defined in [UEP-0016](uep-0016.md).

## Budgeting the Flood beyond a Route {#flood-budget}

Routed hops spend no `FHOPS_REM`, so the whole budget belongs to the flood
that begins where the route ends. A sender sizing `FHOPS_REM` for a
source-routed packet budgets the flood past the last named repeater, not the
route. A packet can so be carried to a distant area and flooded there.

This is why the [flood radius](uep-0014.md#flood-radius) bounds the flood and
not the path. With 15 hints and 15 flood hops, a path from a conforming
sender is at most 31 hops: 15 routed hops, 15 flood hops, and the sender's
own transmission.

How much flood to allow past a known
route is defined in [UEP-0016](uep-0016.md).

## Security Considerations {#security-considerations}

**The route is public.** Every listener learns the repeater hints a packet
will cross, and from them something of where its endpoints are.

**The route is not authenticated.** The Source Route is dynamic, outside the
MIC ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#associated-data)). A forwarder can rewrite,
extend, or empty it, diverting the packet or stopping it. The MIC still
protects what is delivered.

**Named repeaters can drop.** A repeater named in a route can drop the packet
silently, and nothing on the route carries it around that repeater. Recovery
belongs to the sender ([UEP-0016](uep-0016.md)).

**Hint collisions** let repeaters the sender did not name forward the packet.
They cost duplicate transmissions, not misdelivery.

**An empty route's provenance can be forged.** Anyone can add an empty Source
Route to a copy, or strip one, so it is evidence of an explicit path only
from conforming forwarders.

**Remote amplification.** A route plus a flood budget lets one sender start a
flood far from itself. The flood is bounded by `FHOPS`, by duplicate
suppression, and by region policy where it begins.

## Appendix A: Routed-Hop Examples {#routed-vectors}

These examples are informative.

The packet is the unicast of [UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#unicast-vector), from
Node A to Node B, with its options replaced. Every option involved is
dynamic and `FHOPS` and `H` are outside the MIC, so the MIC is unchanged and
verifies under the keys of [UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#keys-vectors) at every
hop. The repeaters are:

| Repeater | Private key | Repeater hint | Default region |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | `51` × 32 | `C0 50` | none |
| R2 | `52` × 32 | `20 12` | none |
| R3 | `53` × 32 | `F8 0C` | `SJC` (`78 53`) |

### A Route, Then a Flood {#hybrid-vector}

Source Route `C0 50 20 12` and `FHOPS` 2/0. The options are `34 C0 50 20 12`.

```text
D1 20 6C 28 FD ED 54 A5 E0 00 00 00 0A 34 C0 50
20 12 FF B7 C4 59 EC 72 BD EA E9 59 DB 86 BA AD
14 9C 92 5F B4 0B
```

After R1, a routed hop. The route loses `C0 50`; `FHOPS` is untouched.

```text
D1 20 6C 28 FD ED 54 A5 E0 00 00 00 0A 32 20 12
FF B7 C4 59 EC 72 BD EA E9 59 DB 86 BA AD 14 9C
92 5F B4 0B
```

After R2, the routed hop that removes the last hint. The option is kept
empty (`30`); `FHOPS` is untouched and no region code is inserted.

```text
D1 20 6C 28 FD ED 54 A5 E0 00 00 00 0A 30 FF B7
C4 59 EC 72 BD EA E9 59 DB 86 BA AD 14 9C 92 5F
B4 0B
```

After R3, the first flood hop. `FHOPS` becomes 1/1 and R3 inserts its default
region after the empty route, as option delta 8 (`82 78 53`).

```text
D1 11 6C 28 FD ED 54 A5 E0 00 00 00 0A 30 82 78
53 FF B7 C4 59 EC 72 BD EA E9 59 DB 86 BA AD 14
9C 92 5F B4 0B
```

The path to Node B, if R3's forward reaches it, is four hops: two routed hops,
one flood hop, and Node A's own transmission.

### A Route without a Flood {#route-only-vector}

The same packet without `FHOPS`, so `H` is clear, with Source Route `C0 50`.

```text
D0 6C 28 FD ED 54 A5 E0 00 00 00 0A 32 C0 50 FF
B7 C4 59 EC 72 BD EA E9 59 DB 86 BA AD 14 9C 92
5F B4 0B
```

After R1:

```text
D0 6C 28 FD ED 54 A5 E0 00 00 00 0A 30 FF B7 C4
59 EC 72 BD EA E9 59 DB 86 BA AD 14 9C 92 5F B4
0B
```

R3, hearing this copy, does not forward it: the route is consumed and the
packet carries no `FHOPS`.
