This UEP defines how nodes find paths to one another and keep them: the
Trace option and the edit each forwarding hop makes to it, beacons and the
schedule they are announced on, when a sender includes a trace and what a
receiver learns from one, the one-hop rule for a node heard directly,
choosing a route and scoping a packet's flood hops to it, routing
acknowledgments, recovery from a route that fails, lost acknowledgments, how
MAC commands that measure or ask about a path are traced and routed, and the
Peer Repeaters commands. UMSH has no packet type for path discovery: paths
are learned from the traffic itself.

## Terminology {#terminology}

**Trace**
: The list of repeaters a packet crossed, most recent first, which each
  forwarding hop extends as [Trace Option](#trace) defines.

**Beacon**
: A broadcast or multicast with an empty payload, sent to announce that its
  source is present ([Beacons](#beacons)).

**Route**
: What a node holds about how to reach a peer: a source route, a flood
  distance, or that the peer is a direct neighbor.

**Flood distance**
: The `FHOPS_ACC` with which a packet from a peer arrived, together with the
  region codes it carried. A peer last heard at flood distance `n` is
  reached by flooding `n` flood hops within those regions.

**Provisional route**
: A path an unsigned beacon suggests to a peer the node holds no route to,
  tried only on the node's own acknowledged sends
  ([Provisional Routes](#provisional-routes)).

**Direct neighbor**
: A node heard off its own transmitter, one [hop](uep-0003.md#terminology)
  away.

**Last hop**
: The link between a destination and the first node that heard it on a
  route to it: the receiving node itself for a direct neighbor, or the
  repeater named last in the route's source route.

**Flood budget**
: The most flood hops a send may use: for a packet, what its sending
  application allows; for a MAC ack, what the destination allows.

**Margin**
: One flood hop a sender adds to a packet's flood hop count beyond what its
  route costs ([The Margin](#margin)).

## Trace Option (7) {#trace}

The Trace option asks every hop that forwards a packet to record itself.

- **Attributes**: forwarding-critical and dynamic
  ([UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#option-attributes)). A repeater that does not
  implement the option does not forward a packet that carries it, so a trace
  never silently skips a hop. Packets without the option are unaffected.
- **Value**: one of the [forms](#trace-forms) below. A sender includes the
  option with an empty value, and each forwarding hop adds an entry as
  [The Forwarding Edit](#trace-edit) defines. A packet without the option
  gains none in transit.
- **Repeats**: a sender MUST NOT include more than one. A receiver reads the
  first and ignores the rest, and a forwarder edits and carries only the
  first.
- **Malformed value**: a value that matches no form cannot be interpreted
  ([UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#criticality)). A repeater does not forward the
  packet. A receiver reads the packet as one without a trace, for route
  learning, path hop count, and acknowledgment routing alike, and still
  delivers it.

### Value Forms {#trace-forms}

| Form | First byte | Length | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complete, with signal | Any | A multiple of 4, including 0 | 4-byte [hop records](#hop-records) |
| Complete, hints only | `0xFE` | Odd | `0xFE`, then a 2-byte repeater hint per hop |
| Truncated | `0xFF` | Odd | `0xFF`, then the 2-byte repeater hints of the most recent hops |

In every form the entries are most recent first. A lone `0xFE` or `0xFF` is
valid. A value whose length is 2 more than a multiple of 4, or whose length
is odd and whose first byte is neither `0xFE` nor `0xFF`, is malformed. The
length tells the hints-only forms from a list of records whose first
repeater hint begins with `0xFE` or `0xFF`.

A complete trace names every hop that forwarded the packet. A truncated
trace names only the most recent: older hops were dropped to fit the frame.

### Hop Records {#hop-records}

```text
+----------+------+-----+
|    RH    | RSSI | SNR |
+----------+------+-----+
    2 B      1 B    1 B
```

- `RH`: the [repeater hint](uep-0004.md#repeater-hint) of the hop that
  forwarded the packet.
- `RSSI`: the negative of the RSSI, in dBm, at which that hop received the
  packet, as an unsigned byte: −90 dBm is written 90. A reading of −1 dBm or
  stronger is written 1, and one weaker than −255 dBm is written 255.
- `SNR`: the SNR at which that hop received the packet, in quarter-dB steps,
  as a signed byte: 7.5 dB is written 30. A reading beyond the byte's range,
  −32 to +31.75 dB, is written as the nearest value it holds: 127 or −128.

A hop that received the packet on an arrival class with no measurement, a
[shared medium](uep-0013.md#shared-medium) or a
[point-to-point](uep-0013.md#point-to-point) link, writes `00 00` for the
two signal bytes. It still writes a record, so that every reading after it
pairs with the right hop. A hop that measured its reception never writes an
`RSSI` of 0, so `00 00` is never a reading. A receiver reads an entry of
`00 00` as unmeasured.

### The Forwarding Edit {#trace-edit}

Every forward of a traced packet, [routed or flood](uep-0013.md#hop-kinds),
adds the forwarding hop to the trace. This is step 5 of the
[edits to the packet](uep-0013.md#edits), the size-adaptive edit, and it
takes the room that steps 1 to 4 leave on the outgoing link
([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#link-fit)).

The repeater computes the exact size of the forwarded packet with each
candidate value, counting the option's whole record: its header, the
extended length byte a value longer than 12 bytes needs
([UEP-0003](uep-0003.md#option-records)), and the value. It takes the first
of these that fits the link's frame limit:

1. Its own entry added in the trace's current form: a 4-byte record before a
   trace with signal, or its 2-byte repeater hint after the first byte of a
   hints-only or truncated trace.
2. For a trace with signal: `0xFE`, its own hint, then every earlier hint.
3. `0xFF`, its own hint, then as many of the earlier hints, most recent
   first, as fit.
4. `0xFF` alone.

If even `0xFF` alone does not fit, the repeater does not forward the packet
on that link, and counts the drop ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#link-fit)).

Forms only degrade. A repeater never adds signal to a hints-only trace, so
the two complete forms never mix, and never turns a truncated trace back
into a complete one.

### Sender Headroom {#trace-headroom}

A sender that includes a Trace option MUST leave at least one byte between
the packet's size and the frame limit, so that a repeater forwarding onto a
link with the same limit can write `0xFF` alone. A repeater that also
inserts or lengthens an option, such as a
[Region Code](uep-0014.md#region-insertion) or a
[Station Callsign](uep-0017.md#station-callsign-edit), needs more room than
that byte, and declines a packet that leaves too
little.

### Reading a Trace {#reading-trace}

A complete trace, with signal or hints only, is a path: read as it stands,
most recent first, it lists the repeaters from the receiver back toward the
packet's originator. A hints-only trace lacks the per-hop signal, and the
receiver still measures the final link itself.

A truncated trace is not a path. A receiver builds nothing from it: no
route, no acknowledgment return route, and no source route made from
observed hints.

A receiver determines a received packet's
[path hop count](uep-0013.md#terminology) as follows, counting an absent
`FHOPS` as an `FHOPS_ACC` of zero:

| The packet carries | Path hop count |
|---|---|
| A complete trace | Its number of entries plus one, or `FHOPS_ACC + 1` if that is larger |
| No complete trace and no Source Route option | `FHOPS_ACC + 1` |
| No complete trace, and a Source Route option, even an empty one | Unknown |

A conforming forwarder never lets the trace count fewer entries than
`FHOPS_ACC`, but the trace is not authenticated, and the larger of the two
is the safer reading.

## Discovering a Path {#discovery}

A node discovers a path with the packets it already sends:

1. **Outbound.** Node A sends Node B a packet with a Trace option and a
   flood hop count wide enough to reach it. Each repeater that forwards it
   adds itself.
2. **Learning.** The trace B receives lists the repeaters most recent first,
   so a complete trace is already the source route back to A
   ([Route Learning](#route-learning)).
3. **Return.** B sends to A along that route. If A's packet requested an
   acknowledgment, B's MAC ack crosses the mesh too, and confirms to A that B
   is reachable.

A's trace teaches B a path to A, and nothing else. B's response teaches A a
path to B only if it carries a trace of its own. When the response is a MAC
ack, the ack is all A receives.

A source-routed packet arrives with its hints consumed, and nothing on it
describes the way back. Every forwarding hop, routed or flood, records itself
in a trace, so a trace restores that path. A destination that holds no route of its own would compose a reply that no
repeater could carry. A sender therefore includes a trace whenever the
destination may not hold a working path back, as
[When to Include a Trace](#including-trace) defines.

## When to Include a Trace {#including-trace}

### Unicast and Group Unicast {#unicast-trace}

A sender SHOULD include a Trace option on a unicast or group unicast when any
of these holds:

1. It holds no route to the destination.
2. The most recent packet it accepted from the destination arrived with
   `FHOPS_ACC` greater than zero, and it has sent the destination no traced
   packet since. A MAC ack that completes a packet sent to the destination
   counts as a packet from the destination.
3. No packet from the destination has arrived with `FHOPS_ACC` of zero, or
   without `FHOPS`, in the last **5 minutes**. A MAC ack counts as in the
   previous condition.
4. The packet is a [Route Retry](#route-retry-edits).

A packet that arrives with `FHOPS_ACC` of zero, off its sender's own
transmitter or along a source route, shows that its sender holds a working
path to the receiver: what a trace would otherwise give the receiver. A
packet that had to flood to arrive shows the opposite. The window bounds how
long that evidence is trusted.

A sender MAY include a Trace option on any other packet, but SHOULD otherwise
prefer the smaller frame.

### MAC Acks {#ack-trace}

A MAC ack teaches the original sender a path to the destination. A
destination SHOULD include a Trace option in a MAC ack when the packet it
acknowledges arrived with `FHOPS_ACC` greater than zero, since the sender
then flooded for want of a path. It MAY include one when the acknowledged
packet carried a Trace option.

### Packets No Repeater May Carry {#unforwardable-trace}

A packet that does not [solicit forwarding](uep-0013.md#delivery-modes)
SHOULD NOT carry a Trace option, whatever else this section says. No
repeater may forward it, so its trace arrives as empty as it left, and its
arrival already shows what an empty trace would.

## Beacons {#beacons}

A **beacon** is a broadcast or multicast whose payload is empty. It announces
that its source is present without carrying data. A broadcast beacon ends
with its options, so it carries no [end marker](uep-0003.md#end-marker). A
multicast beacon carries one, as every [multicast](uep-0005.md#multicast)
does.

A beacon that permits flooding SHOULD carry a Trace option. Much of a
beacon's purpose is to tell distant listeners how the node can be reached,
and any of them may lack a path to it.

### What a Beacon Tells {#beacon-meaning}

A beacon tells its listeners that its source is present, and how many hops
away ([Reading a Trace](#reading-trace)). With a complete trace, it also
names a repeater path that may reach the source, and with signal, the
quality of each hop on it, which is what separates a path that merely works
from one worth using.

A beacon teaches a route to its source only when it authenticates that
source, as [Teaching Packets](#teaching-packets) requires: a beacon with a
verified [frame signature](uep-0007.md#frame-signatures). An unsigned
beacon's source is a claim. Its trace may be shown to a person, and the
repeater it names first was heard directly ([The List](#peer-list)). It
teaches no route, but may suggest a
[provisional route](#provisional-routes) to a peer the node holds no route
to, or put a different route held in doubt.

### The Startup Beacon {#startup-beacon}

A node SHOULD send one beacon when it starts. A node that has just restarted
is the one whose neighbors hold the stalest paths to it. The startup beacon
is not delayed: nodes do not start in unison, so starts are already
scattered.

### Announcing on a Schedule {#beacon-schedule}

A node MAY send beacons on a period of its own, set apart from its
[advertisement](uep-0011.md#advertisements) period. A beacon publishes a path
for a few bytes, while an advertisement is a signed identity, the largest
frame a node sends unprompted. A mesh is best served by refreshing paths
often and restating identities rarely.

A node schedules beacons by the rules
[UEP-0011](uep-0011.md#announcing) gives for advertisements: the period is a
floor, and each beacon is delayed by a random fraction of it.

Identical unsigned beacons from one node share a
[forwarding identity](uep-0013.md#forwarding-identity), so repeaters
suppress a repeat for their [cache lifetime](uep-0013.md#cache-lifetime). A
beacon period shorter than the cache lifetime refreshes the node's direct
neighbors on every period, and the mesh beyond them about once per lifetime.

## Route Learning {#route-learning}

### Teaching Packets {#teaching-packets}

Only an authenticated packet teaches a route to its sender:

- a unicast or group unicast that opens under keys of the sender
  ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#receive-procedure));
- a multicast or broadcast whose
  [frame signature](uep-0007.md#signature-verification) verifies against the
  sender's address;
- a MAC ack whose [`ack_tag`](uep-0007.md#ack-tag) verifies for a packet this
  node sent to the peer and still awaits an acknowledgment for.

An unsigned multicast teaches nothing about the source it names: its MIC
shows only that the sender holds the group key. An unsigned beacon may
instead suggest a [provisional route](#provisional-routes).

A packet teaches only on its first copy: the copy that
[replay detection](uep-0007.md#replay-detection) accepts, or for a MAC ack,
the copy that completes its packet. A duplicate teaches nothing. No MIC
covers the options repeaters rewrite, so anyone who heard a packet can send
it again with a path of their choosing. A packet delivered to the node after
a delay in storage, rather than as it arrived on its link, also teaches
nothing: it shows where its sender was, not where it is.

### What a Packet Teaches {#what-teaches}

A teaching packet teaches the route that applies first:

1. **A complete trace.** The route back is the packet's unconsumed
   [Source Route](uep-0015.md#source-route) hints in reverse order, followed
   by the trace's hints as they stand. With no hints at all, the sender is a
   direct neighbor. Otherwise the hints are a source route to the sender,
   unless there are more than 15 of them, which no
   [source route](uep-0015.md#source-route) may carry: with more than 15,
   the packet teaches nothing. A copy overheard while
   hints remain thus offers the whole path its sender chose, never a shortcut
   past the repeaters still to forward it: that a repeater is heard at the
   destination does not show that it hears the destination.
2. **A truncated trace.** Nothing.
3. **A Source Route option, even an empty one, and no trace.** Nothing. The
   packet spent flood hops only past the end of its route, so its
   `FHOPS_ACC` counts the tail of its path, not the path.
4. **`FHOPS`, and no Source Route option or trace.** A flood distance: the
   packet's `FHOPS_ACC`, with the region codes of every
   [Region Code option](uep-0014.md#region-code) it carried. A distance of
   zero places the sender one hop away.
5. **Neither `FHOPS` nor a Source Route option.** The packet reached the
   receiver off the sender's own transmitter. The receiver SHOULD hold the
   sender as a direct neighbor. This is what an empty trace would show, read
   from the packet's shape, and is why such a packet need not carry one
   ([Packets No Repeater May Carry](#unforwardable-trace)).

A flood distance scopes later floods to the sender to about the right radius
and region, in place of the whole mesh.

### MAC Acks {#ack-learning}

A MAC ack names no source, but its `ack_tag` ties it to the packet it
completes, and so to that packet's destination. It teaches a route to that
peer as any teaching packet does, and its trace can give a node a repeater
path where it held only a direct observation. An ack confirms the exchange,
not the path the packet took: its way back may differ from the way out, so
it need not replace a source route that works.

### Keeping Routes {#keeping-routes}

A learned route applies to all later traffic to its peer: replies,
acknowledgments, and new packets.

- A node MAY replace a route when a later teaching packet offers another.
  Which route a node prefers, and how long it keeps one, is its own policy.
- A node SHOULD discard a route that has proven unreachable
  ([Route Failure Recovery](#route-recovery)).
- A packet whose route ends at the same last hop as the route held refreshes
  the held route's last-hop measurement, whether or not it replaces the
  route.

### Provisional Routes {#provisional-routes}

An unsigned [beacon](#beacons) teaches no route, but its path may still
reach its source. A node MAY take that path as a **provisional route**: one
it tries on its own acknowledged sends, and drops on the first that fails.

**Qualifying beacons.** A beacon qualifies only when all of these hold:

- it was received live, not in storage, and is not a copy of the node's own
  transmission;
- its source is not the node itself, and resolves to exactly one peer the
  node holds by full address: a node hint that matches that peer alone, or
  that peer's full address;
- the peer's identity does not set the `MOB`
  [capability](uep-0011.md#capabilities);
- it carries no Source Route option, and either a complete trace of at most
  15 hints, or no trace and no `FHOPS`.

A qualifying beacon affects the peer only as this section defines. It is not
a packet from the peer for any other rule of this UEP: it refreshes nothing
held about the peer, and makes it no direct neighbor. The signal its trace
records is not used to judge the route.

**The first copy.** A flooded beacon arrives as several copies with
different traces. Only the first copy of a peer's beacon that a node hears
counts. The next to count is one heard once the
[cache lifetime](uep-0013.md#cache-lifetime) has passed, and so from a later
beacon.

**A route held.** A counted beacon whose peer the node holds a route to
suggests nothing. If that route is a source route or a direct neighbor, and
differs from the beacon's path, the node SHOULD hold it in doubt
([The Route in Doubt](#route-doubt)). A flood distance is not put in doubt.

**Suggesting.** Otherwise the beacon suggests the route
[What a Packet Teaches](#what-teaches) would give: its trace's hints as a
source route, or a direct neighbor when there are none. The suggestion
replaces any provisional route held for the peer, unless the node has marked
that path unusable for immediate reuse
([When to Retry](#route-retry-eligibility)). A provisional route expires once
older than the **provisional route lifetime**, measured from when it was
suggested.

**Use.** A node uses a provisional route only for a unicast it originates
that requests an acknowledgment, to a peer it holds no route to, when:

- the unicast is not a group unicast, and does not answer a request the peer
  made;
- its flood budget is not zero;
- the node has chosen no route for it ([Choosing a Route](#choosing-route));
- no other send along the peer's provisional route awaits its
  acknowledgment.

Such a send follows the route with the [margin](#margin) as its only flood
hops, spent past the route's end for a source route, and carries a Trace
option. A packet too large to carry a Trace option does not follow a
provisional route. For every other send, and for every other rule of this
UEP that asks whether the node holds a route to the peer, the node holds
none.

**Acknowledged.** An acknowledgment of a send along a provisional route does
not make it a learned route: a forger on the path that forwards honestly
would otherwise gain a lasting route. The acknowledgment teaches what it
teaches by the ordinary rules, and any route a teaching packet installs
supersedes the provisional one. Otherwise the route stays provisional until
it expires.

**Failed.** When the acknowledgment timeout of a send along a provisional
route runs out, the node drops the route, and takes no suggestion for that
peer for the **suppression period**. It sends the Route Retry an
[assumed route](#route-retry-eligibility) gets. To make room for another
suggestion, a node MUST NOT discard a suppression in force, or a provisional
route that a send awaiting its acknowledgment followed.

The suggested provisional route lifetime is **1 hour**, and the suggested
suppression period **10 minutes**.

## Direct Neighbors {#direct-neighbors}

For routes, the margin, and the peer repeater list, a node treats another
node as a direct neighbor, one hop away, only on a packet that:

- authenticated that node as its sender, as
  [Teaching Packets](#teaching-packets) requires, and was accepted by replay
  detection as it arrived; and
- arrived off that node's own transmitter, so that its
  [path hop count](#reading-trace) is one.

A packet received over a point-to-point link from the node at its other end
arrived off that node's transmitter. A copy of a frame this node's own radio
transmitted never shows a neighbor.

The same holds for an identity ([UEP-0011](uep-0011.md#signatures)). A node
believed on its identity's embedded signature, or heard only through a
repeater, is not thereby a direct neighbor: a signature carries no freshness,
and a captured identity verifies wherever it is put back on the air. Such an
identity describes its node, and is kept, but places the node in no list of
neighbors.

## Choosing a Route {#choosing-route}

A sender that holds a source route to a destination sends along it,
[building](uep-0015.md#building-routes) the Source Route option from it. A
sender MAY choose a route itself, such as one a person picked, in place of a
learned one.

### Scoping Flood Hops {#flood-scope}

A wide flood hop count is a first-contact cost. Once a sender holds a route,
it SHOULD set `FHOPS_REM` to what the route costs, plus the
[margin](#margin) where the far end of the route is in doubt:

| Route | Flood hops |
|---|---|
| Source route | The margin, spent [past the route's end](uep-0015.md#flood-budget) |
| Flood distance `n` | `n` plus the margin |
| Direct neighbor | The margin |
| None | The [flood budget](#terminology) |

The result never exceeds the flood budget, or the
[flood radius](uep-0014.md#flood-radius). A sender whose result is zero
omits `FHOPS` ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#flood-radius)).

### The Margin {#margin}

The margin is one flood hop. It keeps delivery working when the destination
has moved since its route was learned, without a flood across the mesh on
every packet. What decides it is the route's [last hop](#terminology). A
traced path's last hop was measured by the earliest record in the trace,
when the trace still carries signal. A flood distance greater than zero names
no last hop.

A sender decides the margin by the first of these that applies:

1. The sender chose the route itself rather than learning it: it MAY omit
   the margin. This does not apply to acknowledgments
   ([Routing Acknowledgments](#ack-routing)).
2. The destination is a direct neighbor whose last hop was measured strong,
   or that is reached over a shared medium or point-to-point link: it MAY
   omit the margin.
3. The destination's identity sets the `MOB`
   [capability](uep-0011.md#capabilities): it SHOULD include the margin.
4. The last hop was never measured, or was measured weak: it SHOULD include
   the margin.
5. Otherwise it MAY omit the margin.

A last hop was never measured when nothing records a reading of it: a source
route learned from a hints-only trace, or a flood distance greater than zero.
A last
hop recorded as `00 00` by a
[shared medium or point-to-point link](#hop-records) counts as measured and
not weak. The suggested bars are an RSSI stronger than **−75 dBm** for a
strong last hop, and **−96 dBm** or weaker for a weak one.

A direct neighbor measured weak keeps the margin partly because the reverse
direction may need a repeater that the observed packet did not.

### An Optional Hop Is Not a Promise {#optional-hop}

The margin does not imply that a repeater is present. A packet whose route is
a direct neighbor and whose only forwarding solicitation is the margin need
not be confirmed ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#confirmation-scope)): not hearing it
repeated is no reason to retransmit it, whether it is a packet that requests
no acknowledgment or a MAC ack. That an exchange succeeded with the margin
does not show the margin was unnecessary.

## Routing Acknowledgments {#ack-routing}

A destination routes a MAC ack with what the acknowledged packet, and any
earlier one, taught it about the original sender
([Route Learning](#route-learning)), judging the [margin](#margin) from that
route as a sender does:

- **A source route**: the ack follows it, with the margin past its end.
- **A flood distance `n`**: the ack floods `n` flood hops, plus the margin.
  `FHOPS_ACC` thereby scopes the acknowledgment to the radius the packet
  came from.
- **A direct neighbor**: the ack carries the margin as its only flood hops,
  or no `FHOPS` at all.
- **An unknown distance**: a packet that carried a Source Route option and
  no usable trace (none, a truncated one, or one whose way back exceeds 15
  hints) teaches no route. When the destination holds no source route or
  flood distance to the sender, only a direct neighbor or nothing, the ack
  floods at the destination's [flood budget](#terminology), and at least
  `FHOPS_ACC + 1`: a direct observation cannot bound the way back of a route
  the packet consumed. The suggested budget is 5 flood hops, or
  `FHOPS_ACC + 1` when that is larger.
- **No route taught or held**: a packet that teaches nothing and carries no
  Source Route option, such as a flooded packet whose trace arrived
  truncated, still shows how far it flooded. When the destination holds no
  route to the sender, the ack floods `FHOPS_ACC` flood hops, plus the
  margin.

A sender that wants its acknowledgments to cross long source-routed paths
reliably includes a trace ([When to Include a Trace](#including-trace)).

An ack carries the Region Code options of the packet it acknowledges, even
when it also follows a source route, to scope any flooding. When that packet
carried none, an ack scoped by a flood distance carries that distance's
region codes. The acknowledged packet's flood budget governs that packet,
not the way back the destination chooses for its ack.

An ack for a duplicate of a packet already acknowledged
([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#duplicate-acknowledgments)) SHOULD carry the margin
whatever its route: the duplicate shows the earlier ack did not arrive, and a
strong link can still be one-way. A Route Retry duplicate is acknowledged as
[Lost Acknowledgments](#lost-acks) defines instead.

A route the destination chose itself does not exempt its acks from the
margin: how far an acknowledgment reaches is never an application's choice.

## Route Failure Recovery {#route-recovery}

A sender that narrows a packet to a route makes an assumption about the
destination. The assumption may be an explicit source route, or a flood
hop count narrowed because the destination was believed a direct neighbor or
within a known flood distance. Nothing on the wire tells a narrowed flood
from a short one by choice, and both fail the same way from the sender's
side: the acknowledgment does not arrive. An acknowledgment timeout shows that
the exchange failed, not that the way out did.

This section applies to a unicast or group unicast that requests an
acknowledgment. How long a sender waits for an acknowledgment, its
acknowledgment timeout, is its own choice, made to cover the path's forwards
and their [retries](uep-0013.md#forward-retry).

### When to Retry {#route-retry-eligibility}

When a packet's acknowledgment timeout runs out:

- **Assumed route.** If the packet followed a source route or a
  [provisional route](#provisional-routes), or carried fewer flood hops than
  its [flood budget](#terminology), the sender SHOULD treat
  the route as failed: it SHOULD discard the route, or mark it unusable for
  immediate reuse. It SHOULD then send one Route Retry of the packet.
- **No assumed route.** A sender that held no route flooded at its whole
  budget and has no route to discard, but the acknowledgment may have been
  lost on the destination's way back. A MAC ack is never itself
  acknowledged, so the destination cannot learn this on its own. The sender
  SHOULD send one Route Retry if it has heard from the destination recently,
  and SHOULD NOT otherwise. The suggested window is **10 minutes**.
- **Reachability test.** A packet sent to test whether the destination is
  reachable, such as an acknowledged [No-Op](#no-op-retry), SHOULD get its
  Route Retry whether or not the destination was heard recently. Otherwise a
  lost acknowledgment would report a reachable destination as unreachable.

A packet whose flood budget the application set to zero gets no Route Retry:
disabled flooding does not authorize a flooded retry. A packet that already carries a
[Route Retry option](uep-0013.md#route-retry) is never retried again
([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#route-retry)).

### The Retry {#route-retry-edits}

A Route Retry is the same logical packet: the same frame counter, payload,
and MIC. The sender rewrites only fields the
[associated data](uep-0007.md#associated-data) excludes:

- it removes the Source Route option, rather than leaving it empty, so that
  the destination can read the retry's `FHOPS_ACC` as a distance;
- it SHOULD set `FHOPS` to the packet's [flood budget](#terminology), with
  `FHOPS_ACC` zero, setting the `H` bit if the packet carried no `FHOPS`. It
  MUST NOT widen that budget, and a packet that already flooded at it keeps
  it;
- it adds the [Route Retry option](uep-0013.md#route-retry);
- it SHOULD include a Trace option, so that the destination learns a path
  back.

A sender sizes a packet it may retry so that its retry, with these options,
also fits the frame limit. It SHOULD send the retry after a delay drawn
uniformly from the range a [forwarding retry](uep-0013.md#forward-retry)
uses.

The retry is a new [forwarding identity](uep-0013.md#forwarding-identity),
so repeaters that suppressed the original carry it. It is a separate attempt
from the original's [forwarding confirmation](uep-0013.md#confirmation-scope),
and is itself confirmed and retried as any packet is.

### After Recovery {#after-recovery}

An acknowledgment that arrives after a Route Retry confirms that the logical
packet was delivered, not which attempt delivered it. Once the retry's
acknowledgment, or any later packet from the destination, teaches a
replacement route, the sender uses it.

## Lost Acknowledgments {#lost-acks}

A sender sends a Route Retry only once its acknowledgment timeout has run
out. A Route Retry that arrives at the destination as a duplicate of a packet
it has acknowledged therefore suggests that the acknowledgment was lost.

### Re-acknowledging a Route Retry {#reack}

When a Route Retry arrives as a duplicate of a packet the destination has
acknowledged, and [acknowledgment pacing](uep-0013.md#ack-pacing) permits
another acknowledgment, the destination SHOULD acknowledge it again by a
scoped flood rather than along any route it holds, one it chose itself
included:

- `FHOPS_REM` is the retry's `FHOPS_ACC` plus one, at most 15;
- the ack carries the retry's Region Code options and no Source Route;
- the ack carries a Trace option, so that the sender learns a path back;
- the ack carries the Route Retry option
  ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#route-retry)).

The route the lost ack followed may be what failed, so the re-acknowledgment
does not follow it.

The retry is a lost-acknowledgment sign only when its original was
acknowledged. If the destination accepted the original without acknowledging
it, for example a copy overheard before its source route was consumed, the
retry is the first copy eligible for an acknowledgment, and is acknowledged
along the route as usual. A duplicate without the option may have crossed an
acknowledgment in transit, and is acknowledged along the route with the
[margin](#ack-routing).

### The Route in Doubt {#route-doubt}

A Route Retry does not prove the acknowledgment was lost: anyone can add the
option to a copy. A destination SHOULD NOT retire a route on a Route Retry
alone, and does not learn a route from the duplicate
([Teaching Packets](#teaching-packets)). It keeps sending along its route to
the sender, and SHOULD hold that route in doubt until a packet it accepts
from the sender teaches a route ([What a Packet Teaches](#what-teaches)):

- The same route ends the doubt.
- Any other route replaces the one held, however the two compare, unless the
  node chose the held route itself ([Choosing a Route](#choosing-route)).
- An acknowledgment of a packet the destination sent along the route ends
  the doubt.

A packet that teaches no route leaves the doubt in place. The first copy of
an unsigned beacon whose path differs from the route held puts that route in
doubt in the same way ([Provisional Routes](#provisional-routes)).

A forged Route Retry can then do no more than let the sender's next packet
choose the route. A failed route back also shows when the destination's own
sends go unacknowledged, but a destination that sends nothing of its own
never sees that.

A Route Retry that is the first copy of its packet the destination accepts is
an ordinary teaching packet, and the route it teaches MAY replace the route
held.

## MAC Commands {#commands}

### Echo {#echo-trace}

An [Echo Response](uep-0006.md#echo-response) SHOULD carry a Trace option
when its request carried one, unless the response solicits no forwarding
([Packets No Repeater May Carry](#unforwardable-trace)). An echo measures a
path, and a response traced differently from its request measures a
different one. The per-hop signal in the two traces makes the measurement
per hop rather than end to end.

### No-Op {#no-op-retry}

A [No-Op](uep-0006.md#no-op) in a packet that requests an acknowledgment is
retransmitted and recovered like any such packet. As a reachability test, it
gets its Route Retry whether or not the sender has heard from the
destination recently ([When to Retry](#route-retry-eligibility)).

### Identity Request {#identity-request-routing}

These rules add to [Solicitations](uep-0011.md#solicitations):

- **Steered solicitations.** A solicitation may be steered by a Source Route
  to a neighborhood far from the requester. A node MUST NOT answer a
  solicitation whose Source Route option is present and not empty: the
  request is still on its way to the neighborhood it was sent to ask.
- **The steered request's trace.** A steered solicitation reaches its
  neighborhood with its route consumed, and the nodes there hold no route
  back to a requester they may never have heard. A requester that steers a
  solicitation SHOULD include a Trace option.
- **Answering along the trace.** A node that answers a solicitation carrying
  a complete, non-empty trace SHOULD send its answer with a Source Route
  built from the trace, as it stands, without reversal. An answer whose
  route would exceed 15 hints is sent without one, as an answer to an
  untraced solicitation is ([UEP-0011](uep-0011.md#solicitations)).
- **The answer's own trace.** An answer SHOULD carry a Trace option whenever
  it solicits forwarding. The request's trace taught the responder a path,
  and taught the requester nothing: a solicitation names no responder, and an
  answer that arrives with its source route consumed shows the requester no
  way back.
- **Asking a named repeater.** A repeater consumes its hint when it forwards
  a packet, not when it receives one, and it does not answer a solicitation
  whose route still names it. To ask a repeater whose repeater hint the
  requester holds, the requester steers the solicitation to the hop before
  it, with a `FILTER_NODE_HINT` of that repeater hint.

## Peer Repeaters {#peer-repeaters}

The Peer Repeaters commands ask a node for the repeaters it hears directly:
the first hop of every path through it. Both are carried by unicast and group
unicast only ([UEP-0006](uep-0006.md#carriage)), and the response is carried
as [UEP-0005 §response-carriage](uep-0005.md#response-carriage) defines.

### Who Answers {#peer-repeaters-responders}

A repeater SHOULD answer a Peer Repeaters Request from any node. Any other
node, an [adaptive forwarder](uep-0018.md) included, keeps the same list but
is not a repeater its neighbors plan on: it MAY answer, and SHOULD answer
only nodes it trusts, such as those that manage it.

### The List {#peer-list}

A node's peer repeaters are the repeaters it has heard from their own
transmitters. A repeater enters the list in one of two ways:

- **By trace.** A repeater that forwarded a packet the node received prepends
  its entry to the packet's trace, so the first entry of a received trace
  names the repeater whose transmission was just heard. Such an entry is
  known only by its repeater hint.
- **By identity.** A [node identity](uep-0011.md#payload) whose role is
  Repeater or Bridge, or whose capabilities include `REP`, heard as a
  [direct neighbor](#direct-neighbors) on a packet that authenticated its
  node.

Nothing else enters the list: not packets from nodes that are not repeaters,
not identities that arrived through a repeater, and not identities believed
only on their embedded signature. Nor does anything that names the node
itself: a trace whose first entry is its own repeater hint, or its own
identity. A packet received in storage rather than live, or a copy of the
node's own transmission, enters nothing.

A repeater reached over a point-to-point link, such as one attached to the
node, is a peer repeater like any other, and has no signal measurements.

**Proof.** A trace hint is not authenticated, so a repeater the list holds
only by trace is unproven until the node hears it forward a packet the node
itself sent, on a copy that names it as the forwarder: by the first entry of
its trace, or as the source route hint missing from the route the node sent.
When the trace entries are full, a repeater heard only by trace
takes the place of an unproven entry, never of a proven one.

**Attribution.** A trace hint is credited to every entry by identity whose
node hint begins with it and whose identity was heard within the last
**8 hours**, or, failing those, to the only entry by identity whose node hint
begins with it. A credited hint is listed as part of those entries, and a
hint credited to none is listed under its repeater hint alone.

**Lifetime.** An entry by identity counts as heard whenever its identity, a
trace hint credited to it, or a packet it originated is heard from it as a
direct neighbor. It stays listed until **a week** passes without its identity
being heard as a direct neighbor again, whatever else is heard from it.

**Replacement.** When the entries by identity are full, a newly heard
repeater takes the place of, in this order: one not heard in **2 hours**; a
mobile one; one whose role is Repeater or Bridge without the `REP`
capability; one with the `REP` capability and neither role; any other.
Within each class the least recently heard goes first. A newcomer never
takes the place of an entry of a later class than its own.

The durations above are suggested values.

### Encoding {#peer-repeaters-encoding}

Both commands carry [options blocks](uep-0003.md#options) whose option
numbers are local to the command. A receiver ignores an option it does not
recognize, and one whose value has a length the option does not allow.

### Peer Repeaters Request (10) {#peer-repeaters-request}

Asks the destination for one page of its peer repeaters.

| Field | Size | Description |
|---|---:|---|
| Request options | 0+ bytes | An options block, with no end marker |

| Number | Name | Value | Description |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | Nonce | 2 bytes | Copied into the response |
| 1 | Cursor | 0+ bytes | Copied from the Cursor of the previous response. Absent from the first request of a listing |

### Peer Repeaters Response (11) {#peer-repeaters-response}

Answers a Peer Repeaters Request with one page of the list:

```text
+---------+------+---------+------+---------+------+-----+---------+--------+
| OPTIONS | 0xFF | ENTRY 0 | 0xFF | ENTRY 1 | 0xFF | ... | ENTRY N | [0xFF] |
+---------+------+---------+------+---------+------+-----+---------+--------+
```

`OPTIONS` is the response's options block. It is always followed by `0xFF`,
even on a page with no entries. Entries are options blocks separated by
`0xFF`. A sender does not write `0xFF` after the last entry, and a receiver
accepts one there ([UEP-0003](uep-0003.md#end-marker)).

| Number | Name | Value | Description |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | Nonce | 2 bytes | The request's Nonce, unchanged. A responder MUST include it when the request carried one, and only then |
| 1 | Cursor | 0+ bytes | Where the next page begins. Present when entries remain, absent from the last page |
| 2 | Total | 1 byte | The number of entries in the whole list, not the page, saturating at 255. Present in the response to a request without a Cursor, optional otherwise |

The options of an entry:

| Number | Name | Value | Description |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | Node Hint | 2 or 3 bytes | The repeater's [node hint](uep-0004.md#node-hint) when the responder holds it whole, or its 2-byte repeater hint when that is all it has heard. Required |
| 1 | Name | 1–24 bytes | The repeater's [Name](uep-0011.md#name), as its identity gives it |
| 2 | RSSI/SNR | 2 bytes | The most recent reception from the repeater: the negative of its RSSI in dBm as an unsigned byte, then its SNR in quarter-dB steps as a signed byte |
| 3 | Last Heard | 1–2 bytes | Minutes since the repeater was last heard, as a minimal big-endian unsigned integer, saturating at 65535 |
| 4 | Location | 1–7 bytes | The repeater's [location code](uep-0010.md#format), as its identity gives it |
| 5 | Supported Regions | 2 × n bytes | The [region codes](uep-0014.md#region-encoding) of the regions the repeater's [Supported Regions](uep-0014.md#supported-regions) names |

A responder omits an option it has no value for. A peer repeater reached
over a point-to-point link carries no RSSI/SNR option. Both bytes are
written as in a [hop record](#hop-records). A receiver ignores an entry without a valid Node Hint.

Entries carry region codes rather than the region strings of an identity, to
save space; a region's string form, when wanted, is in the repeater's own
identity.

### Paging {#peer-paging}

A list too large for one response is returned a page at a time. The first
request carries no Cursor, and each response that carries one names where a
further request resumes. A requester stops at the response that carries no
Cursor.

A cursor is opaque to the requester, and only the responder gives it
meaning. A cursor resumes the listing in the order its first page began, so
a repeater heard again between requests is neither repeated nor skipped. A
responder that cannot resume that order, because entries have since come or
gone, answers from the start of the list. A responder lists its entries most
recently heard first, and omits an entry too large for any page.

## Security Considerations {#security-considerations}

**Traces are not authenticated.** The Trace option is dynamic, outside the
MIC ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#associated-data)). A forwarder can rewrite,
extend, or truncate it, and steer the route the destination learns. Learning
only from the first authenticated copy of a packet bounds this to the nodes
that carried that copy: a node that replays a packet with a path of its
choosing teaches nothing.

**Unsigned beacons are claims.** An unsigned beacon's source is whatever it
says, and its trace whatever its carriers wrote. It teaches no route. A
forged one can cost each node that holds the peer it names one
acknowledgment timeout and one Route Retry flood per suppression period
([Provisional Routes](#provisional-routes)), and can put a route held in
doubt, which the peer's next authenticated packet settles. A forged path
that delivers keeps the node's acknowledged sends to that peer on it until
the provisional route expires or an authenticated packet teaches a route.
A forged beacon
identical to one a node repeats, carried ahead of the node's own, also
suppresses that node's beacons beyond its direct neighbors for the
[cache lifetime](uep-0013.md#cache-lifetime).

**Topology and location.** A trace discloses the repeaters a packet crossed,
and with signal, how strongly each heard the one before it. Beacons and Peer
Repeaters responses disclose a node's neighborhood. Together they let a
listener map the mesh and estimate where its nodes are.

**Route Retry.** Anyone can add the option to a copy. At the destination, a
forged retry earns at most one paced re-acknowledgment, flooded no further
than the retry came, and holds a route in doubt until the sender's next
packet settles it ([The Route in Doubt](#route-doubt)).

**Peer Repeaters.** A repeater known only by trace is unproven until it
carries the responder's own packet, so a forged trace hint displaces no
proven entry. A response reveals the responder's neighborhood to any node it
answers.

**Margins and recovery** are bounded by the sending application's flood
budget, which recovery never widens.

## Appendix A: Test Vectors {#test-vectors}

Values are hexadecimal. The repeaters are those of
[UEP-0015 Appendix A](uep-0015.md#routed-vectors):

| Repeater | Private key | Address begins | Default region |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | `51` × 32 | `C0 50 C5` | none |
| R2 | `52` × 32 | `20 12 CB` | none |
| R3 | `53` × 32 | `F8 0C CC` | `SJC` (`78 53`) |

### A Traced Beacon {#beacon-vector}

A beacon from Node A of [UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#test-vectors), sent by node
hint with `FHOPS` 3/0 and an empty Trace option (`70`):

```text
C1 30 ED 54 A5 70
```

R1 receives it at −90 dBm and 7.5 dB, and flood-forwards it. `FHOPS` becomes
2/1, and the trace gains the record `C0 50 5A 1E`:

```text
C1 21 ED 54 A5 74 C0 50 5A 1E
```

R2 receives that copy over a point-to-point link, and writes `00 00` for its
signal:

```text
C1 12 ED 54 A5 78 20 12 00 00 C0 50 5A 1E
```

R3 receives that copy at −101 dBm and −4.25 dB (`65 EF`), and inserts its
default region after the trace, as option delta 4 (`42 78 53`):

```text
C1 03 ED 54 A5 7C F8 0C 65 EF 20 12 00 00 C0 50
5A 1E 42 78 53
```

A node that hears R3's forward reads a complete trace of three entries, a
path hop count of four, and a route back to Node A of `F8 0C 20 12 C0 50`.
The beacon is unsigned, so the route is not learned, but a node that holds
Node A and no route to it may take the route as a
[provisional route](#provisional-routes).

Had R1 received the beacon at +3 dBm and −35 dB, its record would be
`C0 50 01 80`: both bytes saturate.

### The Forwarding Edit {#trace-edit-vectors}

R3, which heard the packet at −101 dBm and −4.25 dB, forwards a packet whose
trace is `20 12 00 00 C0 50 5A 1E`. The value it writes depends on the room
left for the whole Trace option record:

| Room (bytes) | Trace value |
|---:|---|
| 13 or more | `F8 0C 65 EF 20 12 00 00 C0 50 5A 1E` |
| 8–12 | `FE F8 0C 20 12 C0 50` |
| 6–7 | `FF F8 0C 20 12` |
| 4–5 | `FF F8 0C` |
| 2–3 | `FF` |
| 0–1 | Not forwarded |

The 12-byte value fits a 13-byte record; a value of 13 bytes or more would
also need an extended length byte. Forwarding the hints-only trace
`FE 20 12 C0 50` instead, R3 writes `FE F8 0C 20 12 C0 50` with 8 or more
bytes of room, and `FF F8 0C 20 12` with 6 or 7.

```text
74 6F 70 12 61 19 11 0C FF 02 20 12 22 6E E0 12
01 2C 22 78 53
```

A later request, with Nonce `12 35` and a Cursor of `00 07 02` taken from an
