This UEP defines how repeaters carry packets beyond one hop: flood and
source-routed forwarding and which kind a hop is, the forwarding procedure
and the stages later UEPs add to, duplicate suppression, the pending forward
and the events that defer, cut short, abandon, confirm, and retry it, the
Route Retry option, and acknowledgment pacing.

Flood hops are defined by [UEP-0013](uep-0013.md), routed hops by
[UEP-0014](uep-0014.md), and traces by [UEP-0015](uep-0015.md). A repeater
MUST implement UEP-0013, UEP-0014, and UEP-0015. Source Route, Region Code,
and Trace are forwarding-critical, so a repeater lacking any of them could not
forward the packets that carry them.

## Terminology {#terminology}

**Forward**
: A repeater's retransmission of a packet it received, edited as the
  [forwarding procedure](#forwarding-procedure) defines. Also the act of
  making one.

**Routed hop**
: A forward by the repeater named by the first hint of a packet's Source
  Route ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#routed-hop)). Also called a source-routed hop.

**Flood hop**
: A forward that spends one from `FHOPS_REM`
  ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#flood-hops)). Every forward that is not a routed
  hop is a flood hop.

**Path hop count**
: The number of [hops](uep-0003.md#terminology) a packet traveled: its
  routed hops, plus its flood hops, plus one for the originator's
  transmission. A packet heard directly from its originator has traveled one
  hop.

**Originator**
: The node that built the packet, also called its original sender.

**Forwarding identity**
: The value a repeater recognizes a packet by across forwards, as
  [Forwarding Identity](#forwarding-identity) defines.

**Pending forward**
: A forward a repeater has committed to and not yet finished with: *unaired*
  until its first transmission, then *aired* while it awaits
  [confirmation](#forwarding-confirmation).

**Arrival class**
: How the copy a repeater is forwarding reached it, as
  [Arrival Classes](#arrival-classes) defines.

**Further along**
: One copy of a packet is further along than another when it carries fewer
  source-route hints, or as many with greater `FHOPS_ACC`. A copy that is not
  further along is a copy *from behind*.

## Delivery Modes {#delivery-modes}

A packet is delivered in one of four ways:

- **Direct**: it carries no `FHOPS` and no non-empty Source Route, and only
  the nodes that hear its originator receive it.
- **Flooded**: it carries [`FHOPS`](uep-0005.md#fhops), and repeaters
  forward it outward, bounded by its flood hop budget.
- **Source-routed**: it carries a [Source Route](uep-0014.md#source-route),
  and the repeaters the route names forward it in turn.
- **Hybrid**: it carries both. It follows the route first, then floods
  ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#route-end)).

Endpoints choose how their own traffic is routed, and repeaters carry out
that choice. A packet **solicits forwarding** when it carries `FHOPS_REM`
greater than zero or a non-empty Source Route.

## Hop Kinds {#hop-kinds}

A forward is a routed hop or a flood hop, never both. These rules apply to
flood hops only:

- flood hop accounting ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#fhops-accounting));
- region policy ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#region-policy));
- checks that UEPs add to [Flood-Only Checks](#flood-checks), such as
  signal-quality thresholds;
- region insertion ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#region-insertion));
- the contention delay ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#contention-window));
- the [`D_ack` hold-back](#ack-hold-back).

A repeater MUST NOT apply them to a routed hop, including the routed hop that
removes the last hint of a Source Route.

## Routing Invariants {#routing-invariants}

1. **Forwarded packet types.** Every packet type
   [UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#packet-types) defines is forwarded by this
   procedure: broadcast, MAC ack, unicast, multicast, and group unicast. A
   packet of a reserved type, or of a version a repeater does not implement,
   is discarded and not forwarded, as UEP-0005 defines. A packet type a later
   UEP defines is forwarded as that UEP states.
2. **Own transmissions.** A repeater MUST NOT forward a packet it transmitted
   itself: doing so wastes airtime, and a trace would record a hop that never
   happened.
   - A full-address source equal to one of the repeater's own addresses
     identifies such a packet.
   - A node hint does not, since another node's address can share it
     ([UEP-0004](uep-0004.md#matching)). A repeater recognizes its own
     packets sent with a node hint by entering each one's forwarding identity
     in its [duplicate cache](#duplicate-cache) as it transmits it.
3. **Packets that have arrived.** A repeater MUST NOT forward a unicast or
   group unicast that a key of one of its own identities opened, whether or
   not replay detection then accepted it. The packet has reached its
   destination, and so has every later copy of it.
   - A destination hint that matches one of the repeater's node hints is not
     enough. The packet may be for another node that shares the hint, and is
     forwarded unless one of the repeater's keys opens it.
   - A multicast the repeater opens is still forwarded to the group's other
     members.
   - A MAC ack whose `ack_tag` verifies for one of the repeater's own pending
     sends ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#ack-tag)) has also arrived, and is not
     forwarded.
   - A unicast or group unicast that [UEP-0009](uep-0009.md#retired-sessions)
     discards as addressed to a retired session has arrived, and is not
     forwarded. It is matched by both addresses, not by one hint.
4. **Every forward changes the packet.** A routed hop shortens the Source
   Route, and a flood hop changes `FHOPS`. These [edits](#edits) do not make
   a new logical packet.
5. **Route Retry.** A forwarder MUST NOT add a
   [Route Retry option](#route-retry). Only the originator adds it.
6. **`FHOPS`.** A forwarder does not add or remove `FHOPS`, and changes it
   only as [UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#fhops-accounting) defines.
7. **Acknowledgments.** Repeaters forward MAC acks like any other packet, and
   never generate them ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#acknowledgments)).
8. **Two identities.** Whether a packet is new is decided by its destination,
   by replay detection ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#frame-counters)). Repeaters
   recognize a packet by its [forwarding identity](#forwarding-identity),
   which stays the same across every edit a forwarder may make. Duplicate
   suppression and [forwarding confirmation](#forwarding-confirmation) use
   the same forwarding identity, so a node recognizes its own packet carried
   onward after the next repeater has edited it.

## Duplicate Suppression {#duplicate-suppression}

Each repeater keeps a cache of the forwarding identities of the packets it
has forwarded or transmitted, and does not forward a packet whose identity
the cache holds.

### Forwarding Identity {#forwarding-identity}

A packet's forwarding identity is a digest of its fields that forwarding
never changes, together with whether the packet carries a
[Route Retry option](#route-retry):

- **Unicast, multicast, and group unicast**: the digest is the packet's MIC.
  The MIC covers every static field and none of the dynamic ones
  ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#associated-data)), so forwarding leaves it
  unchanged.
- **Broadcast and MAC ack**: these carry no MIC. The digest is a hash,
  computed by the repeater, over exactly the fields a MIC would cover:
  - the packet type and the `S` bit;
  - the static options;
  - for a broadcast, `SRC` and the payload;
  - for a MAC ack, its 8-byte trailer.

  It excludes `FHOPS`, the `H` bit, and every dynamic option. The hash need
  not be cryptographic. Each repeater computes the identities of the packets
  it sends and hears itself, so the choice of hash is local.

Route Retry gives a packet two forwarding identities: the original attempt
and one re-attempt.

Copies of a packet that differ only in dynamic options or `FHOPS`, such as a
Region Code, Source Route, or Trace, share a forwarding identity. Where
repeaters insert different region codes into copies of one packet, the flood
may carry more than one, and each later repeater forwards the first copy it
hears ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#region-insertion)).

A shorter MIC ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#mic-size)) makes a false match between
different packets more likely. A deployment that uses 4- or 8-byte MICs
accounts for this when sizing the cache.

### The Cache {#duplicate-cache}

The cache has a fixed size the implementation chooses. When it is full, the
oldest entry is evicted to make room.

An entry is 4 to 16 bytes, so generous sizing is cheap. A repeater SHOULD
hold at least 32 entries. The suggested size is 64, and a busy mesh or one of
large diameter may want 128 or more.

### Cache Lifetime {#cache-lifetime}

Entries MUST age out. Capacity alone does not bound how long an identity is
suppressed: a node that repeats an identical packet, such as a beacon, would
otherwise be suppressed for as long as a quiet repeater runs.

- A repeater SHOULD discard an entry once it is older than the **cache
  lifetime**, measured from when the identity was first inserted. The
  suggested cache lifetime is one hour.
- An entry for a MAC ack SHOULD live for at least the
  [confirmation timeout](#confirm-timeout) plus `T_frame`, which covers the
  late flood copies of one acknowledgment and a forwarded MAC ack's single
  [retry](#forward-retry). It MAY live as long as the cache lifetime. The
  suggested lifetime is the confirmation timeout plus `T_frame`, which frees
  the entry for traffic that outlives it.
- A repeat of an identity the cache holds MUST NOT extend its entry's
  lifetime. Otherwise a node repeating itself within the lifetime would hold
  its own suppression open indefinitely.

## Route Retry Option (6) {#route-retry}

A flag: the originator is attempting the same logical packet again after its
acknowledgment timed out. How and when a sender uses it is defined by
[UEP-0015](uep-0015.md).

- **Attributes**: non-critical and dynamic
  ([UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#option-attributes)).
- **Value**: a sender includes the option with no value. A receiver treats
  it as present whatever its value, and a forwarder carries its value
  unchanged.
- **Repeats**: a sender MUST NOT include more than one. A receiver reads the
  first and ignores the rest, and a forwarder carries only the first.
- **At most once**: a sender MUST NOT send more than one Route Retry of a
  logical packet, which bounds a packet's forwarding identities to two.

With the option present, repeaters treat an authenticated packet as a
distinct forwarding attempt, as [Forwarding Identity](#forwarding-identity)
defines, though its MIC and frame counter are unchanged. Repeaters that
suppressed the original as a duplicate therefore carry the re-attempt.

At the destination, the option does not make a new logical packet. Replay
detection decides whether the packet is accepted
([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#frame-counters)). A destination that acknowledges a
packet carrying the option with a MAC ack MUST include the option in the
ack, so that repeaters that carried the first acknowledgment carry this one
too. The option is outside the ack's associated data, so its `ack_tag` is
unchanged.

Every field a sender rewrites for a retry is one the
[associated data](uep-0007.md#associated-data) excludes, so the retry
carries the original MIC.

Anyone who heard a packet can add the option to a copy, so its presence
proves nothing about who sent the copy.

## Arrival Classes {#arrival-classes}

How a repeater treats a copy depends on how the copy reached it. A UEP that
carries packets over a medium or link states which class its arrivals belong
to.

### Measured {#measured}

Received on a radio, which measured its RSSI and SNR. Every rule applies:
checks that need a measurement, ranked contention
([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#contention-window)), and
[giving way](#give-way).

### Shared Medium {#shared-medium}

Received on a medium that every node on it hears equally and that carries no
measurement, such as an IP multicast group.

- Checks that need a measurement do not apply.
- Contention is unranked ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#contention-window)).
- A copy received this way does not make a pending forward give way.
- A copy received this way confirms a forward.

### Point-to-Point {#point-to-point}

Received over a link that only this repeater heard, such as one to an
attached host or a bridge tunnel.

- There is no contention to resolve, and no contention delay.
- Checks that need a measurement do not apply.
- A copy received this way does not make a pending forward give way.
- A copy received this way confirms a forward.

How a trace records such a hop is defined by [UEP-0015](uep-0015.md).

A copy of a frame this repeater's own radio transmitted is not an arrival
class. [Eligibility](#eligibility) defines how it is handled.

| Class | Measurement checks | Contention | A copy makes a pending forward give way | A copy confirms a forward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Measured | Apply | Ranked | Yes | Yes |
| Shared medium | Do not apply | Unranked | No | Yes |
| Point-to-point | Do not apply | None | No | Yes |

## Forwarding Procedure {#forwarding-procedure}

A repeater runs this procedure for every packet it receives, however the
packet arrived, after or alongside the
[receive procedure](uep-0007.md#receive-procedure). Delivering a packet
locally does not consume it: a broadcast or multicast the repeater delivers
is still forwarded.

The procedure has five stages, taken in order. Later UEPs add checks and
edits to named stages, and never reorder the stages. If any check in stages 1
to 3 declines and records, the forwarding identity is recorded whatever other
checks decline, so the order of checks within a stage does not matter.

### 1. Eligibility {#eligibility}

The repeater declines the packet when:

- **Not forwarded**: its type is not forwarded
  ([Routing Invariants](#routing-invariants)), or
  [UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#options) says to discard it.
- **Own radio's copy**: the copy is one this repeater's own radio just
  transmitted. A repeater that shares its radio with another stack, such as an
  attached host, receives a copy of each frame that stack transmits. It MUST
  NOT forward it, and it MUST record the packet's forwarding identity as if it
  had forwarded it, since a neighbor's forward of the same packet arrives
  shortly after. This is a [decline and record](#stage-outcomes).
- **Own or arrived**: the repeater transmitted the packet itself, or the
  packet has arrived ([Routing Invariants](#routing-invariants)).
- **Duplicate**: its forwarding identity is in the
  [duplicate cache](#duplicate-cache). The copy may also make a pending
  forward [give way](#give-way).
- **Forwarding-critical option**: it carries a forwarding-critical option the
  repeater does not recognize or cannot interpret
  ([UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#criticality)). Delivery-critical options do not
  affect forwarding.
- **Local policy**: its local policy excludes the packet. A repeater MAY
  decline packets its policy excludes, such as a broadcast
  [Identity Request](uep-0010.md#identity-request), particularly one whose
  filters could solicit many replies.

[UEP-0016](uep-0016.md) adds amateur radio station rules to this stage, and
[UEP-0017](uep-0017.md) adds a battery rule.

### 2. Routed or Flood Hop {#hop-kind}

- If the packet carries a non-empty
  [Source Route](uep-0014.md#source-route), the forward is a routed hop when
  its first hint is this repeater's repeater hint, matched as
  [UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#route-matching) defines. Otherwise the repeater
  declines.
- If the Source Route is absent or empty, the forward is a flood hop.

A routed hop skips stage 3.

### 3. Flood-Only Checks {#flood-checks}

A flood hop is declined unless it passes every check that UEPs add to this
stage. [UEP-0013](uep-0013.md) adds two:

- flood hop accounting ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#fhops-accounting));
- region policy ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#region-policy)).

Signal-Quality Filtering adds signal-quality thresholds, and
[UEP-0017](uep-0017.md) adds checks of its own.

### 4. Edits to the Packet {#edits}

The repeater edits the packet in this order:

| Step | Edit | Hops | Defined in |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Flood hop accounting | Flood | [UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#fhops-accounting) |
| 2 | Source Route hint removal | Routed | [UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#routed-hop-edit) |
| 3 | Region Code insertion | Flood | [UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#region-insertion) |
| 4 | Fixed-size edits added by later UEPs, such as Station Callsign | As the UEP defines | [UEP-0016](uep-0016.md) |
| 5 | Size-adaptive edits, such as Trace | Both | [UEP-0015](uep-0015.md) |

The order governs how the forwarded packet is [measured](#link-fit). On the
wire, the repeater:

- encodes the options in order of increasing number
  ([UEP-0003](uep-0003.md#option-records));
- carries every option it does not edit unchanged, value included, an
  unrecognized dynamic option among them;
- carries only the first occurrence of an option whose UEP says a forwarder
  carries only its first;
- leaves every static option untouched
  ([UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#dynamic-static));
- copies the fields before the options other than `FHOPS`, and everything
  after the options, verbatim;
- writes the [end marker](uep-0003.md#end-marker) as UEP-0003 defines, and
  places the options as [UEP-0005](uep-0005.md#options-region) locates them.

#### Fitting the Outgoing Link {#link-fit}

Every decline in stages 1 to 3 is decided before any edit is made.

The repeater computes the exact size of the forwarded packet on the link it
transmits the forward on:

1. after the fixed-size edits, steps 1 to 4, including any option it
   inserts;
2. then the size-adaptive edits take the room that is left, as their UEPs
   define.

The limit is the link's frame limit, such as the
[LoRa MTU](uep-0011.md#framing). If the packet does not fit, even with the
size-adaptive edits at their smallest, the repeater does not forward it and
counts the drop. This is a decline, and records nothing. A packet that a
Region Code insertion makes too large is dropped in the same way. How a
repeater carries frames onto other interfaces is defined by the UEPs for
those interfaces.

### 5. Scheduling {#scheduling}

- **Routed hop**: the forward is not held. It is transmitted under normal
  [channel access](uep-0011.md#channel-access).
- **Flood hop**: the forward waits the contention delay for its arrival class
  ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#contention-window)), plus `D_ack` where the
  [hold-back](#ack-hold-back) applies, plus any delay a later UEP adds to
  this stage, such as [UEP-0017](uep-0017.md)'s offset. It is then
  transmitted under normal channel access.

A repeater SHOULD go on transmitting other frames while a forward waits. A
repeater with no room to hold the forward declines it.

### Stage Outcomes {#stage-outcomes}

| Outcome | Forwarding identity |
|---|---|
| **Forward** | Recorded when the repeater commits to the forward: after stages 1 to 4 pass, the packet fits the link, and the forward is queued in stage 5. This is before the forward is transmitted, so that later copies are not forwarded while it waits. |
| **Decline** | Not recorded. |
| **Decline and record** | Recorded, though nothing is forwarded. Only where a stage says so: the own radio's copy in [Eligibility](#eligibility), and the declines [UEP-0017](uep-0017.md) adds. |

## Acknowledgment Hold-Back {#ack-hold-back}

A repeater that flood-forwards a packet requesting an acknowledgment (`UNAR`
or `GUAR`) MUST hold it back by at least
[`D_ack`](uep-0011.md#ack-protection), in addition to its contention delay.
The destination's [immediate acknowledgment](uep-0011.md#immediate-ack)
then has the channel first, and repeaters can
[overhear it](#overheard-acks) before their forwards air.

- A routed hop is never held back. A packet with source-route hops still
  pending draws no immediate acknowledgment
  ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#pending-route-ack)), and every routed hop receives
  the packet with its own hint still pending.
- The hold-back applies whatever the forward's arrival class.
- When a forward [gives way](#give-way), the recomputed delay includes
  `D_ack` when the overheard copy requests an acknowledgment and carries no
  source-route hops, since that copy may itself draw an immediate
  acknowledgment.

## The Pending Forward {#pending-forward}

A forward is unaired until it is first transmitted, and aired from then until
it is confirmed or given up. Only a routed hop has an aired phase that awaits
confirmation ([Who Listens](#confirmation-scope)). Its forwarding identity
stays recorded unless the table says otherwise.

| Event | Unaired forward | Aired forward |
|---|---|---|
| A measured copy further along is heard | Flood hop: [gives way](#give-way), and is abandoned on the third copy | — |
| An acknowledgment of it is overheard | [Cut short](#overheard-acks) to one sensing attempt | [Confirmed](#forwarding-confirmation) |
| Channel access is spent ([UEP-0011](uep-0011.md#backoff)) | Given up; identity [released](#releasing-key) | Given up |
| A [confirming copy](#confirming-copy) is heard | — | Confirmed |
| The confirmation timeout expires | — | [Retried](#forward-retry) |
| Retries are exhausted | — | Ends unconfirmed |

### Giving Way {#give-way}

An unaired flood forward gives way when the repeater receives, on the
[measured](#measured) class, a copy with the same forwarding identity that
another node forwarded and that is [further along](#terminology) than the
copy this repeater received. A copy from behind, such as the originator's own
retransmission, does not count. Routed forwards do not give way.

- On each of the first two such copies, the repeater SHOULD defer: it
  recomputes its [stage 5](#scheduling) delay as on first receipt, measured
  on the overheard copy, including `D_ack` as
  [Acknowledgment Hold-Back](#ack-hold-back) defines, and restarts the wait
  from when the copy was received.
- On the third, it SHOULD abandon the forward.

An abandoned forward keeps its forwarding identity: the packet went onward
without it.

Giving way governs only the repeater's first decision. Once a forward has
aired, confirmation and retry govern it. [UEP-0017](uep-0017.md) changes
which copies count against an adaptive forwarder's pending forward.

### Overheard Acknowledgments {#overheard-acks}

A MAC ack's `ack_mic` ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#ack-tag)) can be read without
keys, and survives forwarding. A repeater that overhears a MAC ack, or an
[Ack MIC option](uep-0007.md#ack-mic-option), whose `ack_mic` equals the
first four bytes of the MIC of one of its pending forwards of a `UNAR` or
`GUAR` packet treats it as follows:

- **Unaired**: the repeater does not remove the forward. It SHOULD cut the
  forward's channel-access budget to a single
  [sensing attempt](uep-0011.md#channel-sensing), keeping its place and its
  delay. If the channel is then clear, the forward airs. If it is busy, the
  forward is given up and [releases its identity](#releasing-key). A forward
  cut short is not listened for or retried once it airs.
- **Aired**: the acknowledgment confirms it.

A repeater cannot tell an Ack MIC option on a
[Verify Frame](uep-0007.md#verify-frame) from one that acknowledges, and
treats both alike: either shows that the named packet reached its
destination.

An overheard Ack MIC option SHOULD also cut short an unaired forward of a MAC
ack with the same `ack_mic`, since the packet carrying the option delivers
the same acknowledgment. An overheard MAC ack never cuts short the forward of
another MAC ack: a second copy of one ack would cut short the first.

The cut acts on the forwards pending when the acknowledgment is heard, and
records nothing. A Route Retry copy received later has its own forwarding
identity, and is queued with a full budget.

An overheard `ack_mic` is a hint, not proof: anyone who saw the packet can
send it ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#security-considerations)). A forged
acknowledgment costs the sender at most one retry, and only on a busy
channel.

### Releasing the Identity {#releasing-key}

A forward given up before it ever aired, for any reason other than
[giving way](#give-way), SHOULD release its forwarding identity, so that a
later copy is forwarded. Such reasons include channel access spent
([UEP-0011](uep-0011.md#backoff)), duty-cycle withholds included, and a
forward cut short that found the channel busy. Left in place, the identity would suppress every
copy of a packet the repeater never carried. An aired forward keeps its
identity.

## Forwarding Confirmation {#forwarding-confirmation}

A node confirms that a packet it transmitted went onward by hearing a later
copy of it carried onward, or an acknowledgment of it.

### Who Listens {#confirmation-scope}

- **Originator.** An originator of a unicast or group unicast that solicits
  forwarding SHOULD listen for confirmation, and an originator of a broadcast
  or multicast that solicits forwarding MAY.
  - For a source-routed packet, it listens for the first named repeater.
  - For a flooded packet, it listens for any node.
  - Confirmation applies whether or not the packet requests an
    acknowledgment. A sender that requested one then awaits it. A sender that
    did not is finished when it hears the packet carried onward, and if its
    retries run out, the send ends with no failure signal.
- **No forwarding solicited.** An originator's packet that does not solicit
  forwarding confirms nothing, and the originator MUST NOT retransmit it for
  lack of confirmation. A [Route Retry](#route-retry) is a separate attempt
  ([UEP-0015](uep-0015.md)).
- **Repeater on a routed hop.** It listens for the next named repeater. The
  routed hop that removed the last hint listens only when the packet requests
  an acknowledgment: the destination's acknowledgment confirms it, as does
  any repeater heard flooding the packet onward. Any other packet, a MAC ack
  among them, it transmits once.
- **Flood repeaters** MUST NOT retry. Several may forward the same flood, and
  none has a designated next hop.
- **Forwarded MAC acks.** A destination that sends a MAC ack carrying a
  Source Route or `FHOPS` listens for its first hop and retries on silence,
  which spares the sender a full retransmission when only the ack's first hop
  failed. The destination, and a repeater on a routed hop forwarding such an
  ack, retry it at most once: an identical retry falls within the ack's
  [cache lifetime](#cache-lifetime) at a repeater that did carry the first.
  The destination stops retrying an aired ack once it sends a packet carrying
  an Ack MIC option for the same packet.

### The Confirming Copy {#confirming-copy}

A copy confirms a transmission when it has the same forwarding identity and
matches the transmitted packet on every byte a forwarder may not change: the
FCF apart from `H`, the fields before the options other than `FHOPS`, the
static options, and everything after the options. A copy with the same
identity and different bytes confirms nothing.

For a repeater's aired forward, only a copy [further along](#terminology)
than the forward confirms it. A copy from behind confirms nothing.

A copy received on any arrival class confirms.

### Confirmation Timeout {#confirm-timeout}

The confirmation timeout runs from the end of the node's transmission. It
MUST cover the next hop's worst-case intentional forwarding delay, the
airtime of its forward, and a guard margin:

```text
confirm_timeout = (W_max + 2 × W_jitter) + D_ack + T_frame + T_frame
```

`W_max + 2 × W_jitter` bounds the wait of a repeater that heard the packet
below the contention band ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#contention-window)), and
`D_ack` is included where the [hold-back](#ack-hold-back) applies. The first
`T_frame` is the forward's airtime, and the second is the guard margin. A
node MAY always include `D_ack`. A UEP that adds a delay to
[Scheduling](#scheduling) states how `confirm_timeout` covers it. With
UEP-0013's defaults and the suggested `D_ack` of `0.25 × T_frame`
([UEP-0011](uep-0011.md#ack-protection)), `confirm_timeout` is
`2.95 × T_frame`.

### Retry {#forward-retry}

If the confirmation timeout expires without confirmation, the node SHOULD
retry after a delay drawn uniformly from `[0, T_frame]`. The delay does not
grow with each retry: it only decorrelates the retries of different nodes.
The retry is byte-identical, and is transmitted under normal
[channel access](uep-0011.md#channel-access).

A node MUST NOT retry a transmission more than 3 times, and retries a
[forwarded MAC ack](#confirmation-scope) at most once.

## Acknowledgment Pacing {#ack-pacing}

[Duplicate Acknowledgments](uep-0007.md#duplicate-acknowledgments) makes a
duplicate eligible for acknowledgment. Eligibility is not obligation:
flooding delivers one transmission as several copies, and a copy of a
transmission already acknowledged shows nothing was lost.

A destination SHOULD NOT acknowledge the same packet more than once per
[confirmation timeout](#confirm-timeout), measured from its most recent
acknowledgment of that packet. Copies arriving within it share the
acknowledgment already sent, and a sender that lost its acknowledgment cannot
retransmit before its own confirmation timeout has run out.

Accepting a packet does not start the window, so the first eligible
acknowledgment may be sent at once. A destination that accepted a packet
while source-route hops were pending may acknowledge the first copy that
arrives with the route consumed ([UEP-0014](uep-0014.md#pending-route-ack)).
Acknowledging a duplicate neither accepts nor delivers it again
([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#duplicate-acknowledgments)).

How a re-acknowledgment after a Route Retry is routed is defined by
[UEP-0015](uep-0015.md).

## Security Considerations {#security-considerations}

**Amplification.** One transmission becomes up to one forward per repeater
per flood hop, bounded by `FHOPS` ([UEP-0013](uep-0013.md#flood-radius)),
duplicate suppression, and region policy. Anyone can start a flood at the
cost of one transmission.

**Cache eviction.** A node that transmits many distinct packets evicts
entries from repeaters' caches, so older packets are forwarded again.
Destinations still reject the replays
([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#replay-detection)); the cost is airtime. Entries
expire so that a repeater cannot be made to suppress a packet indefinitely.

**Predictable identities.** The forwarding identity of a broadcast or MAC ack
follows from its content. A node that transmits a packet's content first
takes its identity, and repeaters suppress the genuine packet as a
duplicate. A broadcast repeated identically is suppressed for the cache
lifetime.

**Route Retry.** Anyone can add the option to a copy. It buys at most one
extra forwarding identity per packet, and a destination never treats it as a
new delivery.

**Forged confirmation.** A forged copy that appears further along, or a
forged `ack_mic`, stops a node's retries early. The cost is bounded to
forwarding retries. An acknowledgment is authenticated only by its
`ack_tag` ([UEP-0007](uep-0007.md#ack-tag)).

**Short MICs.** A short MIC makes collisions between the forwarding
identities of different packets more likely, and a collision suppresses a
packet that was never forwarded.

**Withholding.** A repeater that does not forward a unicast it opened shows
observers that it is the destination, as its immediate acknowledgment does.

**Dynamic fields.** `FHOPS`, `H`, and dynamic options are not authenticated,
and a malicious repeater can alter them. [UEP-0013](uep-0013.md) and
[UEP-0014](uep-0014.md) describe the effect for the fields they define.
